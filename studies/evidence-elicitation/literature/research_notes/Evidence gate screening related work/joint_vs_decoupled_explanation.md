# Joint vs. Decoupled Prediction and Explanation in LLMs/NLP (as of 2026-10-09)

Scope: whether asking a model to produce rationales, quotes, chain-of-thought (CoT), or structured outputs changes its predictions; accuracy cost of faithfulness-by-construction (select-then-predict); LLM-era two-call "decide, then extract" designs; cost accounting; rationale failure as a deferral signal.

Verification legend used throughout: **[full text]** = body/tables read (arXiv HTML, ar5iv, or full-text preprint); **[abstract only]** = only the landing page/abstract was read, numbers beyond the abstract are not verified. Venues are reported only when stated on the page fetched; otherwise marked "(venue not verified)".

Papers read beyond the abstract (8): Wiegreffe et al. 2021; Camburu et al. 2018 (e-SNLI); Jain et al. 2020 (FRESH); Lei et al. 2016; Tam et al. 2024; Sprague et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024; Windisch et al. 2026 (medRxiv); plus Lu et al. 2025 (ranking), Chegini et al. 2025 (Reasoning's Razor), Patil 2025, Cohen et al. 2025, and the dottxt rebuttal.

## Key Question 1: Is there direct evidence that adding an evidence-quotation requirement (not just CoT) to a classification prompt changes label distributions or accuracy? (Paired designs preferred)

### Takeaway
Direct paired evidence is thin but exists: one 2026 medRxiv preprint ran a within-model paired comparison (label-only vs. label+verbatim quote, identical inputs, separate API calls) on 200 oncology abstracts with three frontier models and found model-dependent effects: coverage fell modestly for all three models, UNCLEAR/abstention rates rose, macro-F1 moved slightly up for two models and down for one, and majority-vote accuracy dropped significantly for Claude Opus 4.5 (0.785 to 0.725, McNemar p = 0.009) but not for GPT-5.2 or Gemini 3 Flash. The older, well-powered evidence is about free-text rationales rather than quotes: joint self-rationalization (I->OR) costs ~0.1 points on e-SNLI but 4-6 points on CoS-E relative to predict-only, and extractive-rationale bottlenecks (select-then-predict) cost 0-9 F1 points depending on the dataset.

### Cited Findings
- **Paired quote-requirement study (biomedical classification).** Windisch et al., "Show Your Work: Verbatim Evidence Requirements and Automated Assessment for Large Language Models in Biomedical Text Processing," medRxiv preprint, posted March 2026. **[full text]** Design: 200 oncology RCT abstracts (2005-2023) each classified under a baseline prompt (label only) and an evidence-required prompt (label + verbatim quote), identical input text, separate API requests (the evidence condition could not see the baseline answer), three repeats per condition; models GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Flash, Claude Opus 4.5 at default settings. — [medRxiv full text](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
  - Macro-F1 on non-UNCLEAR outputs, baseline -> evidence-required: GPT-5.2 0.910 -> 0.916; Gemini 3 Flash 0.933 -> 0.945; Claude Opus 4.5 0.828 -> 0.777. — [same source](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
  - Majority-vote accuracy (McNemar): GPT-5.2 0.785 -> 0.775 (p = 0.724, n.s.); Gemini 3 Flash 0.930 -> 0.910 (p = 0.453, n.s.); Claude Opus 4.5 0.785 -> 0.725 (p = 0.009, significant decrease). — [same source](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
  - Coverage (non-UNCLEAR, valid): GPT-5.2 86.2% -> 84.3%; Gemini 98.3% -> 92.8%; Claude 96.0% -> 94.5%. UNCLEAR (abstention) rates rose: GPT-5.2 13.8% -> 15.7%; Gemini 0.2% -> 2.2%; Claude 3.8% -> 5.5%. Invalid outputs: Gemini 1.5% -> 5.0%; GPT-5.2 0.0% both; Claude 0.2% -> 0.0%. — [same source](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
  - Label stability (Fleiss' kappa across 3 repeats), baseline vs. evidence-required: GPT-5.2 0.969 vs 0.967; Gemini 0.891 vs 0.829; Claude 0.905 vs 0.928. — [same source](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
  - Quote validity: quotes returned in 84.3% (GPT-5.2), 93.8% (Gemini), 94.5% (Claude) of runs; mechanically valid (exact substring after whitespace normalization) in 83.3%, 91.2%, 91.2%; "correct and mechanically valid" 77.0%, 86.2%, 70.8%. Quote stability across repeats (word-level Jaccard): GPT-5.2 0.896, Claude 0.897, Gemini 0.664. — [same source](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
  - Semantic support (LLM judge): only 48.0%-78.8% of evidence-bearing predictions judged supported (GPT-5.2 73.8-78.8%; Claude 67.2-73.5%; Gemini 48.0-59.0%). Restricting to judge-supported predictions raised macro-F1 (e.g., GPT-5.2 0.92 -> 0.95; Gemini 0.94 -> 0.95; Claude 0.78 -> 0.88 for the representative judge) at the cost of coverage. The paper does not report per-class label distribution shifts, token counts, cost, or latency. — [same source](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
- **Joint free-text rationalization vs. predict-only (fine-tuned T5).** Wiegreffe, Marasović & Smith, "Measuring Association Between Labels and Free-Text Rationales," EMNLP 2021 (per arXiv comments). **[full text]** Task accuracy: I->O (predict only) vs I->OR (joint): e-SNLI 90.95 vs 90.81; CoS-E v1.0 69.16 vs 64.84; CoS-E v1.11 61.75 vs 55.61. Authors: "adding rationalization results in some loss in accuracy." Joint beats the I->R;R->O pipeline on every dataset: e-SNLI 90.81 vs 89.11; CoS-E v1.0 64.84 vs 53.47; CoS-E v1.11 55.61 vs 45.45. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12762); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.12762)
- **e-SNLI: predict-and-explain costs nothing; explain-then-predict costs ~2.3 points.** Camburu et al., "e-SNLI," NeurIPS 2018 (per arXiv comments). **[full text]** SNLI test accuracy: InferSent baseline 84.01 (SD 0.25); PredictAndExplain 83.96 (SD 0.26); ExplainThenPredict-Seq2Seq 81.59 (SD 0.45); ExplainThenPredict-Attention 81.71 (SD 0.36). Human-judged explanation correctness (first 100 test examples): PredictAndExplain 34.68% (over the 80 correctly labeled), ExplainThenPredict-Seq2Seq 49.8%, -Attention 64.27%. Authors describe the explanation bottleneck as "sacrificing a bit of performance" for more trust that predictions are made for the right reasons. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01193); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.01193)
- **Few-shot self-rationalization plausibility ceiling.** Marasović et al., "Few-Shot Self-Rationalization with Natural Language Prompts," NAACL Findings 2022 (per arXiv comments). **[abstract only]** Average human-assessed plausibility of generated explanations is at most 51% (GPT-3) vs. 76% for human explanations. No statement about label-accuracy cost on the page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08284)
- **JSON-mode (constrained) output helps classification but hurts reasoning.** Tam et al., "Let Me Speak Freely? A Study on the Impact of Format Restrictions on Performance of Large Language Models," arXiv 2408.02442v3 (Oct 2024; venue not verified). **[full text]** Classification gains under JSON-mode on DDXPlus: Gemini 1.5 Flash 41.6 -> 60.3; GPT-3.5 Turbo 44.1 -> 55.5; Claude 3 Haiku 33.8 -> 52.0; hypothesis: constraining the answer space reduces answer-selection errors. Reasoning losses: GPT-3.5 Turbo GSM8K 76.6 (text) -> 49.3 (JSON-mode); Last Letter 56.7 -> 25.2; Claude 3 Haiku GSM8K 86.5 -> 23.4 with JSON schema. Parsing errors are not the main cause (LLaMA-3-8B: 0.148% parse-error rate yet 38.15-point gap on Last Letter). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442)
- **Rebuttal: the format effect is largely a prompt/schema-ordering artifact.** Will Kurt (.txt/dottxt), "Say What You Mean: A Response to 'Let Me Speak Freely'," blog post (undated). Criticisms: mismatched prompts across NL vs JSON conditions; JSON prompt never mentioned JSON/schema; the "perfect" AI parser (Claude 3 Haiku) scored below a flexible regex (0.57 vs 0.61); JSON-mode conflated with constrained generation. Re-run on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (unstructured vs structured): GSM8K 0.77 vs 0.78; Last Letter 0.73 vs 0.77; Shuffled Objects 0.41 vs 0.44. Schema placed a "reasoning" field before "answer". — [blog.dottxt.ai](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)
- **LLM annotation: showing reasoning vs. labels moves annotators differently.** A pre-registered study with 410 crowdworkers found LLM suggestions "significantly changed the label distribution" and inflated evaluated model performance. **[search snippet only; not verified]** — [arXiv 2507.15821](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15821)

### Inferences
- The only paired quote-vs-no-quote classification study found is Windisch et al. 2026; its headline is model-dependent: a requirement to quote shifts some mass toward abstention (UNCLEAR) and, for one model, significantly lowers accuracy while leaving the others statistically unchanged. The "evidence gate" (keep only judge-supported predictions) functions as selective prediction: F1 up, coverage down.
- Combining Tam et al. (JSON-mode helps classification by constraining answer space) with Windisch (quote requirement adds an extra generative sub-task) suggests two opposite forces: format constraints on the *label* help; added *generation* obligations (quote, rationale) tend to cost coverage and sometimes accuracy.
- The fine-tuning-era literature consistently shows a monotone ordering: predict-only >= predict-and-explain (joint) > explain-then-predict (pipeline/bottleneck) in label accuracy, with the gap widening on reasoning-heavy tasks (CoS-E) vs. NLI.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that reports per-class label-distribution shifts under a quote requirement; Windisch et al. report only aggregate accuracy/F1/coverage/abstention changes.
- No peer-reviewed (non-preprint) paired study of quote-requirement effects on classification labels with frontier models was found; Windisch et al. is a medRxiv preprint.
- The social-science annotation line (LLM justification effects on human annotators) was seen only via search snippets and is about human labelers, not the model's own predictions.

## Key Question 2: What do select-then-predict / FRESH-style papers report about the accuracy cost of faithfulness-by-construction, and does any LLM-era work revisit this with two separate calls (decide, then extract)?

### Takeaway
Faithfulness-by-construction (predict only from an extracted rationale) costs 0-9 macro-F1 points vs. full-text models in FRESH (largest on SST: .81 vs .90; near-zero on AgNews, Movies, MultiRC) and ~4-5 MAP points vs. the best full-input baseline in Lei et al. 2016; FRESH's decoupled three-stage pipeline beats end-to-end REINFORCE/HardKuma rationale models and is far more stable. LLM-era work that cleanly runs "decide, then extract in a separate call" and reports the accuracy delta is sparse: the closest are a GEM 2025 two-stage extractive-rationale calibration study (rationale before vs. after prediction) and the 2026 "Format Tax" / dottxt line showing that decoupling reasoning from formatting into a second pass recovers accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **FRESH (decoupled extractor + independent classifier).** Jain, Wiegreffe, Pinter & Wallace, "Learning to Faithfully Rationalize by Construction," ACL 2020 (venue per my prior knowledge; page did not state). **[full text]** Three independent components: a support model (BERT) yields feature scores; an extractor discretizes (contiguous span or top-k); a *separate* classifier is trained and tested only on the extracted rationale, so predictions are faithful by construction. Macro-F1 (Table 2), Full text / FRESH best / Lei et al. best / Bastings et al. best: SST .90/.81/.74/.60; AgNews .94/.91/.92/.77; Evidence Inference .73/.68/.53/.50; Movies .95/.94/.87/n.r.; MultiRC .68/.67/.64/.44. Rationale budget 10-30% of input (average human rationale length where available, else 20%). FRESH needs no REINFORCE hyperparameter search and has lower seed variance than Lei et al. Human eval: annotators classified from FRESH contiguous rationales with 92% accuracy on Movies (Lei: 88%) and 69% on MultiRC (Lei: 57%). The paper does not claim faithfulness is free; it says FRESH "performs nearly as well as Full text." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00115); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00115)
- **Original select-then-predict.** Lei, Barzilay & Jaakkola, "Rationalizing Neural Predictions," EMNLP 2016 (per arXiv comments). **[full text]** Generator selects fragments; encoder predicts from fragments only; trained jointly with REINFORCE-style regularizers. Beer reviews: rationales are ~13-14% (appearance) and ~7% (smell, palate) of words; rationale precision vs attention baseline: appearance 96.3% vs 80.6%, smell 95.1% vs 88.4%, palate 80.2% vs 65.3%; MSE of rationale model reported only graphically ("close to the best encoder run"). AskUbuntu retrieval (test MAP): full body 53.0, full title 60.0; rationale (11.6% of words) 54.6; rationale (32.8%) 55.6, i.e., ~4.4-5.4 MAP below the best full-input baseline. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04155); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1606.04155)
- **Pipelines vs joint for free-text rationales.** Wiegreffe et al. 2021 **[full text]**: the I->R;R->O pipeline loses 1.7 points on e-SNLI and 10-11 points on CoS-E relative to the joint model (numbers in KQ1); authors attribute pipeline failure to low-quality rationales and the failure of the sufficiency assumption on reasoning tasks (free-text rationales, unlike extractive ones, are not guaranteed to be sufficient). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.12762)
- **Explain-then-predict bottleneck in e-SNLI** costs ~2.3-2.4 points vs. predict-and-explain (83.96 vs 81.59/81.71) but roughly doubles human-judged explanation correctness (34.68% -> 49.8%/64.27%). **[full text]** — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.01193)
- **Modular (frozen explainer + finetuned predictor) vs joint, LLM-era.** Yeo, Satapathy, Goh & Cambria, "How Interpretable are Reasoning Explanations from Prompting Large Language Models?", NAACL Findings 2024 (per DOI listing). **[Section 2 read; Figure 1 numbers not extractable]** On CSQA and OBQA, a PINTO-style modular setup (frozen LLM explainer, finetuned predictor conditioned on explanation + context) vs. a single LLM trained to jointly produce explanation and answer: the joint approach "scores higher on both accounts of faithfulness and utility" (faithfulness = performance drop when the explanation is swapped; utility = LAS), attributed to a single model being "in better control of aligning its explanation to the resultant outcome"; the gap is described as marginal; no task-accuracy claim is made. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11863); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11863v3)
- **LLM-era two-stage extractive rationales (prompting only).** dela Cruz, Hendrickx & Larson, "Improving Large Language Model Confidence Estimates using Extractive Rationales for Classification," GEM 2025 workshop (ACL Anthology 2025.gem-1.49, pp. 549-560). **[abstract only]** Compares one-stage (rationale generated with the prediction) vs two-stage rationale-guided confidence calibration on disaster-tweet classification with four off-the-shelf LLMs; extracting rationales both before and after the prediction improves confidence estimates; replacing valid extractive rationales with irrelevant ones significantly lowers confidence. Accuracy deltas for E->P vs P->E are not on the abstract page. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.gem-1.49/)
- **Decoupling reasoning from formatting (two-pass) recovers accuracy.** Lee, D'Antoni & Berg-Kirkpatrick, "The Format Tax," arXiv 2604.03616 (Apr 2026). **[abstract only]** Six open-weight and four API models, four formats (JSON, XML, LaTeX, Markdown); most of the loss comes from format-requesting *instructions* before any decoder constraint; "reason-then-format" (freeform first, reformat in a second pass) or extended thinking in one generation "substantially" recovers accuracy; recent closed-weight models show little or no format tax. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03616)
- Tam et al. 2024's own NL-to-Format condition (answer in natural language, then convert) performed "nearly identically" to unrestricted natural language (slight decrease for LLaMA-3-8B). **[full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442)
- **Decomposition can raise accuracy for small models (extraction, not classification-with-evidence).** Cohen et al., "Small Models, Big Results: Achieving Superior Intent Extraction through Decomposition," arXiv 2509.12423 (Sep 2025). **[full text tables]** Gemini Flash 8B, BiFact F1: Mind2Web E2E-FT 0.653 vs Decomposed-FT 0.752; AndroidControl (dataset labels) 0.565 vs 0.596; (expert labels) 0.590 vs 0.701. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12423)

### Inferences
- The classical result is that decoupling (FRESH) *beats* joint end-to-end rationale learning (Lei et al.) in both accuracy and stability, while still trailing a full-text classifier by a dataset-dependent margin; i.e., the cost is of the bottleneck, not of decoupling per se.
- For LLMs, the closest analogue to "decide then extract" in the literature is "reason freely then format" (Tam NL-to-Format; Format Tax; dottxt), which uniformly reports that the second pass is near-free in accuracy. A "label-first, then extract evidence" design is structurally the predict-then-explain ordering that Camburu/Wiegreffe found preserves accuracy but yields less faithful/plausible explanations.
- Yeo et al. 2024 is the one LLM-era data point directly comparing modular vs joint on faithfulness; it favors joint (marginally), consistent with Wiegreffe's label-rationale association results for joint T5 models.

### Gaps
- No LLM-era paper was found that runs the exact design "call 1: label; call 2 (separate context): extract supporting quote" and reports accuracy/label-shift vs. a single joint call. The Windisch et al. design is paired but joint (label + quote in one call).
- Figure-only numbers (Yeo et al. Fig. 1; Lei et al. Fig. 2 MSE curves) could not be extracted.
- The GEM 2025 paper's full tables (E->P vs P->E accuracy) were not accessible from the Anthology landing page in this session.

## Key Question 3: What is known about cost accounting for two-call pipelines (tokens, latency) versus single joint calls?

### Takeaway
Formal cost accounting is rare. The one paper with an explicit table (Cohen et al. 2025, intent extraction) estimates a decomposed pipeline at ~2-3x the cost of a single end-to-end call (input 1839 -> 2103 tokens, output 20 -> 622 tokens, latency 0.24 -> 0.6 s), with a latency-optimized variant matching the single call's latency at ~2.1x cost. Reranking papers quantify the reasoning overhead only indirectly (rationale length ~398 -> 172 tokens after GRPO; "substantially higher inference costs" without latency figures). The quote-requirement and format papers report no token/latency numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Decomposed vs single call, explicit table.** Cohen et al. 2025 (Table 4, per intent description): E2E single call: 1839 input tokens, 20 output tokens, est. cost 191.9 (paper's unit), est. latency 0.24 s, 1 call. Decomposed-FT: 2103 input, 622 output, cost 600, latency 0.6 s (one summary call per interaction plus a fusion call; call count not stated). Decomposed-FT latency-optimized: 2009 input, 514 output, cost 406.5, latency 0.24 s. Authors state Decomposed-FT costs about 2-3x the small-model baselines. **[full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12423)
- **Reasoning rerankers: cost acknowledged, not measured.** Lu et al., "Rethinking Reasoning in Document Ranking: Why Chain-of-Thought Falls Short," arXiv 2510.08985 (Oct 2025), ICLR 2026 poster per iclr.cc. **[full text]** States reasoning models incur "substantially higher inference costs" but reports no latency; the quantitative proxy is rationale length, which GRPO reduces from 397.7 to 172.3 tokens on average. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08985v1); [ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006933)
- **Listwise reasoning reranker vs pointwise reasoning reranker latency.** ReasonRank abstract claims "much lower latency than the pointwise reranker" (Rank1), with no numbers on the abstract page. **[abstract only]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07050)
- **Rank1** distills >600k R1 reasoning traces on MS MARCO for a pointwise reranker; reports that quantized versions retain performance with less compute/memory; no per-query latency on the abstract page. **[abstract only]** — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18418)
- **Format/quote papers report no cost.** Windisch et al. 2026: no token counts, API costs, or latency reported. **[full text]** — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt). Chegini et al. 2025 (Reasoning's Razor): no cost/latency figures; notes only that Think-On generates reasoning before the decision. **[full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21049v1)
- **Reformatting as a cheap corrective second call.** Tam et al. 2024 note parsing errors can be reduced by a corrective second prompt that reformats malformed output; dottxt's rebuttal caps the reasoning field (e.g., 250 characters) to bound runtime. **[full text / blog]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442); [blog.dottxt.ai](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)

### Inferences
- For a decide-then-extract design where call 2 re-sends the document, the dominant added cost is the repeated input (document) plus the extra round trip; the extra output (a short quote) is small. Cohen et al.'s table is the only concrete reference point and shows input overhead of ~14% and latency roughly 2.5x unless calls are parallelized/optimized.
- Reranking results indicate that the token cost of reasoning (hundreds of tokens per candidate) is paid without accuracy benefit in several 2025 studies; the same accounting logic applies to per-item rationales in classification.

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head cost/latency benchmark of "single joint label+quote call" vs. "label call + separate quote call" was found.
- Prompt-caching effects on two-call pipelines are discussed only in practitioner blogs (not cited here as they are not primary sources).

## Key Question 4: Any work treating rationale failure as evidence of prediction unreliability (link to deferral)?

### Takeaway
Yes, in three forms: (a) Ye & Durrett 2022 train a calibrator on explanation-reliability features to improve post-hoc accuracy; (b) Windisch et al. 2026 use quote validity (substring check) and an LLM semantic-support judge as a selective-prediction gate, raising macro-F1 at the cost of coverage (and note that the model's own UNCLEAR option is the abstention route); (c) Patil 2025 documents a "support-contra asymmetry" in which explanations accompanying wrong predictions cite more contradicting cues, though the author stops short of proposing a deferral rule. The GEM 2025 paper links extractive rationales to better verbalized confidence. No paper was found that formally evaluates "abstain iff rationale fails verification" as a selective-classification policy with risk-coverage curves.

### Cited Findings
- **Explanation reliability as calibrator input.** Ye & Durrett, "The Unreliability of Explanations in Few-shot Prompting for Textual Reasoning," NeurIPS 2022 (per arXiv page). **[abstract only]** Four LLMs (OPT, GPT-3 davinci, text-davinci-001, text-davinci-002) on three QA/NLI datasets; explanations give small-to-moderate accuracy gains for three models and larger gains for text-davinci-002; generated explanations may not entail the prediction or be factually grounded in the input "even on simple extractive tasks"; calibrators trained on automatically extracted explanation-reliability scores improve post-hoc performance across all datasets. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03401)
- **Quote validity and semantic support as a gate.** Windisch et al. 2026 **[full text]**: quotes failing substring checks are logged as invalid and excluded from "correct and mechanically valid"; mechanically valid quotes do not guarantee semantic support (48.0-78.8% judged supported); restricting to judge-supported predictions raised macro-F1 (GPT-5.2 0.924-0.947; Gemini 0.932-0.951; Claude 0.856-0.917 depending on judge) with reduced coverage; the authors suggest routing failures to manual review but did not test that workflow; no automated retry for invalid quotes. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)
- **Support-contra asymmetry in explanations of wrong predictions.** Patil, "Support-Contra Asymmetry in LLM Explanations," arXiv 2510.21884v2 (revised Apr 2026), HPE. **[full text]** DeepSeek-R1 at temperature 0 on IMDB (96.5% acc), AG News (84.7%), WikiOntology (96.9%); explanations compared with TF-IDF logistic-regression/SVM lexical cues. At k=5 (token matching, LR), support-cue rate for correct vs incorrect predictions: WikiOntology 0.515 vs 0.302; AG News 0.534 vs 0.472; IMDB 0.277 vs 0.212; contra-cue rate: 0.120 vs 0.211; 0.154 vs 0.247; 0.130 vs 0.198. Asymmetry statistic 0.304 / 0.265 / 0.134. The paper does not propose using this to flag unreliable predictions and cautions the metric measures alignment to an external lexical baseline, not internal faithfulness. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21884v2)
- **Extractive rationales improve verbalized confidence.** dela Cruz et al., GEM 2025 **[abstract only]**: rationales before and after prediction improve confidence estimates; swapping in irrelevant rationales significantly lowers confidence; framed as reducing overconfidence "that could signal hallucination." — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.gem-1.49/)
- **Reasoning hurts the low-FPR operating point that deferral systems care about.** Chegini et al. (Apple), "Reasoning's Razor," arXiv 2510.21049 (Oct 2025). **[full text]** Safety and RAG hallucination detection; Think-On (CoT) raises overall accuracy (up to +11.1% for fine-tuned models) but hurts recall at low false-positive rates: fine-tuned safety model TPR@1%FPR ~40.0% Think-Off vs 13.8% Think-On; QwQ-32B token-based TPR@1%FPR 30.6% (Off) vs 16.8% (On). Explanation: reasoning pushes predictions to extreme confidence so false positives become inseparable from true positives; verbalized confidence gets zero recall at 1% FPR on several datasets; equal-weight ensembling of On/Off scores matched or beat the better single mode on most datasets (CovidQA 79.2% vs 70.6%). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21049v1)
- **Unfaithful CoT under bias (why rationale checks are needed).** Turpin et al., "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think," arXiv 2305.04388 (NeurIPS 2023 per my prior knowledge; not stated on page). **[abstract only]** GPT-3.5 and Claude 1.0; accuracy drops by as much as 36% across 13 BIG-Bench Hard tasks when few-shot options are reordered so the answer is always "(A)"; CoT explanations rationalize the biased answer without mentioning the bias; social-bias task shows stereotype-consistent justifications. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388)
- **Faithfulness of CoT varies by task and model size.** Lanham et al., "Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning," arXiv 2307.13702 (Jul 2023). **[abstract only]** Interventions include adding mistakes and paraphrasing the CoT; models vary in how much they condition on the CoT; "as models become larger and more capable, they produce less faithful reasoning on most tasks we study." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702)
- **Faithfulness vs plausibility framing.** Jacovi & Goldberg, "Towards Faithfully Interpretable NLP Systems," ACL 2020. **[abstract only]** Argues for distinguishing criteria, surveys faithfulness evaluation around three assumptions, and calls for "discarding the binary notion of faithfulness in favor of a more graded one." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03685)
- **Leakage-adjusted simulatability.** Hase, Zhang, Xie & Bansal, "Leakage-Adjusted Simulatability," EMNLP Findings 2020. **[abstract only]** LAS measures how well explanations help an observer predict the model's output while controlling for label leakage; one rationalizing method reaches roughly human-level LAS on CoS-E and e-SNLI. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04119)

### Inferences
- The literature supports a two-tier gate: (1) mechanical verifiability (substring match) is cheap and catches 6-17% of outputs (Windisch), but (2) semantic support fails for a further 21-52% of mechanically valid quotes, so a substring check alone is a weak reliability signal.
- Reasoning's Razor implies that if deferral is implemented via a confidence threshold at low FPR, adding CoT/rationale generation can *reduce* the usable signal; token-level probabilities from a direct-answer call were the better confidence source in that study.
- Patil's asymmetry is a candidate feature for a rationale-based unreliability detector but is unvalidated as a selective-prediction policy.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports risk-coverage or selective-accuracy curves for the policy "defer when the rationale/quote fails verification."
- Ye & Durrett's calibrator details (features, gains) were not verified beyond the abstract.

## Key Question 5 (context): Does CoT/reasoning help or hurt classification, judgment, and reranking tasks?

### Takeaway
Meta-analytic and controlled evidence converges: CoT reliably helps only on math/symbolic/logical tasks; on commonsense, knowledge, and soft-reasoning classification it is neutral on average and sometimes harmful (drops up to -24 points for specific model/dataset pairs); CoT can impair implicit-pattern classification (up to -36.3 points for o1-preview vs zero-shot GPT-4o); and in 2025 reranking studies, reasoning-augmented rerankers consistently underperform direct rerankers (by 4-9 NDCG@10 pointwise) because reasoning breaks calibration and polarizes relevance scores.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta-analysis + 20 datasets x 14 LLMs.** Sprague et al., "To CoT or Not to CoT? Chain-of-thought helps mainly on math and symbolic reasoning," arXiv 2409.12183 (ICLR 2025 per my prior knowledge; not stated on page). **[full text]** Screened 4,642 papers (ICLR/EACL/NAACL 2024), kept 110 with 1,218 CoT-vs-direct comparisons. Literature averages: symbolic +14.2, math +12.3, logical +6.9; all other categories 56.8 (CoT) vs 56.1 (direct). Own zero-shot runs: Math 22.6% -> 50.2% and Symbolic 45.2% -> 56.1% average; commonsense (CSQA, PIQA, SiQA), WinoGrande, ARC, AGI LSAT show little to no gain. ~95% of MMLU's CoT gain comes from questions containing "=". Table 7 zero-shot CoT decreases: BiGGen Bench Qwen2-7B 69.9 -> 46.2 (-23.7); ContextHub Deductive L1 Mistral-7B 67.3 -> 50.3 (-17.0); MuSR Team Allocations Phi-3-Small 61.6 -> 47.2 (-14.4); MuSiQue Qwen2-72B 69.0 -> 56.3 (-12.7); BiGGen Bench GPT-4o-mini 77.7 -> 70.3 (-7.4); many sub-6-point decreases on commonsense/RC/knowledge sets. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12183)
- **CoT hurts where deliberation hurts humans.** Liu, Geng, Wu, Sucholutsky, Lombrozo & Griffiths, "Mind Your Step (by Step)," arXiv 2410.21333 (venue not verified). **[full text]** Implicit statistical learning: o1-preview -36.3 points vs zero-shot GPT-4o; GPT-4o -23.1; Claude 3 Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3.1 70B -6 to -9; Claude 3.5 Sonnet unchanged. Facial recognition: GPT-4o -12.8, Claude 3 Opus -14.4, Gemini 1.5 Pro -11.4. Classifying data with exceptions: rounds-to-learn rose 331% (GPT-4o), 178% (Claude 3.5 Sonnet), 129% (Claude 3 Opus). Three other tasks (logical inconsistency, spatial intuition, feature aggregation) showed no harm because the human constraint does not transfer to LLMs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21333v1)
- **Zero-shot CoT origin.** Kojima et al., "Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners," NeurIPS 2022 (per page). **[abstract only]** "Let's think step by step": MultiArith 17.7% -> 78.7%, GSM8K 10.4% -> 40.7% (text-davinci-002); the abstract lists only arithmetic/symbolic/logical gains. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)
- **Reasoning hurts rerankers (controlled SFT/RL comparison).** Lu et al. 2025, ICLR 2026 poster. **[full text]** Qwen3-4B/8B; MS MARCO training (pointwise: Rank1 corpus ~386k pairs; listwise: ReasonRank corpus ~13k sets; traces from DeepSeek-R1); BRIGHT and BEIR NDCG@10 over BM25 top-100. Pointwise: Direct beats Reason by +9.0 (4B) and +6.1 (8B) on BRIGHT; +5.3 / +4.3 on BEIR. Listwise: Direct beats Reason by +0.4 to +1.7 (BRIGHT) and +0.3 to +1.9 (BEIR). Calibration ECE (BEIR, 8B): Direct 0.106 vs Reason 0.141; reasoning biases pointwise models toward the positive class (TPR up, TNR down). Listwise reasoning: higher training-fit mean but larger variance (82.57 +/- 3.2 vs 80.41 +/- 2.1). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08985v1); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=txmqENuRcc)
- **Disabling reasoning at inference improves a reasoning reranker.** Jedidi, Chuang, Glass & Lin, "Don't 'Overthink' Passage Reranking: Is Reasoning Truly Necessary?", arXiv 2505.16886 (May 2025). **[abstract only]** Under identical training, non-reasoning pointwise rerankers (StandardRR) generally outperform reasoning rerankers (ReasonRR); ReasonRR-NoReason (reasoning disabled) is "surprisingly more effective" than ReasonRR; attributed to reasoning pushing toward polarized relevance scores that miss partial relevance. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16886)
- **Pro-reasoning reranker papers (for balance).** Rank-R1 (Zhuang, Ma, Koopman, Lin, Zuccon; arXiv 2503.06034, Mar 2025) **[abstract only]**: RL-trained reasoning reranker; matches SFT in-domain with 18% of training data; "largely outperforms" zero-shot and SFT on out-of-domain complex queries (TREC DL, BRIGHT). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06034). ReasonRank (Liu et al., arXiv 2508.07050, Aug 2025) **[abstract only]**: listwise, cold-start SFT + RL; "outperforms existing baselines significantly" and claims lower latency than the pointwise reasoning reranker. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07050). Rank1 (Weller et al., arXiv 2502.18418, Feb 2025) **[abstract only]**: first reranker trained for test-time compute; >600k R1 traces. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18418). Lu et al. 2025 note such claims "are rarely supported by fair comparisons against non-reasoning baselines." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08985v1)
- **Judgment tasks: explanation improves LLM-judge correlation; auto-CoT does not reliably.** Chiang & Lee, "A Closer Look into Automatic Evaluation Using Large Language Models," EMNLP Findings 2023. **[abstract only]** G-Eval's auto-CoT "does not always make G-Eval more aligned with human ratings"; numeric-only output is "suboptimal"; asking the LLM to explain its ratings consistently improves correlation with humans. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05657)
- **Precision-sensitive classification.** Chegini et al. 2025 (Reasoning's Razor): see KQ4; Think-On raises accuracy but lowers TPR at 1% FPR by ~2-3x. **[full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21049v1)

### Inferences
- For a screening/classification gate evaluated at a strict operating point, the 2025 evidence (Reasoning's Razor; reranking calibration results) suggests direct answering with token-level confidence may dominate CoT/rationale-first prompting; where CoT helps, the gain is concentrated in symbolic sub-problems.
- The reranking debate is a clean natural experiment in joint-vs-decoupled: adding reasoning tokens before the relevance judgment changes score distributions (polarization, positive-class bias), not just accuracy, which is the same mechanism that would shift label distributions in classification.

### Gaps
- Venue for Sprague et al. and Liu et al. not confirmed from fetched pages.
- No 2026 paper was found that directly extends Sprague's meta-analysis to the quote/evidence-citation condition.

## Key Question 6 (context): Structured-output / format constraints and prediction changes

### Takeaway
The 2024 claim that constrained JSON output degrades reasoning (Tam et al.) has been substantially qualified: the degradation is largely driven by prompt wording and by schemas that force the answer before the reasoning (100% of GPT-3.5 JSON-mode reasoning responses put "answer" before "reason"); re-runs with reasoning-first schemas show parity or gains (dottxt), and a 2026 study attributes most of the loss to format instructions rather than decoding constraints, with closed-weight models showing little or no tax. For classification, constraining the output space helps (DDXPlus +11 to +19 points).

### Cited Findings
- Tam et al. 2024 **[full text]**: three modes (JSON-mode constrained decoding; FRI format-restricting instructions; NL-to-Format). "Stricter format constraints generally lead to greater performance degradation in reasoning tasks." Key-ordering finding: "100% of GPT 3.5 Turbo JSON-mode responses placed the 'answer' key before the 'reason' key," skipping CoT. Removing the schema from the prompt raised averages and lowered variance for several models. No single structured format consistently won across models on classification. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442)
- dottxt rebuttal **[blog, full]**: see KQ1; structured >= unstructured on all three reasoning tasks for Llama-3-8B-Instruct when prompts match and the schema puts reasoning first. — [blog.dottxt.ai](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)
- "The Format Tax" (Lee, D'Antoni, Berg-Kirkpatrick, Apr 2026) **[abstract only]**: format-requesting instructions cause most of the loss before any decoder constraint; reason-then-format or extended thinking recovers most accuracy; closed-weight models mostly unaffected. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03616)
- Sprague et al. 2024 note that even where CoT is used, answer-extraction/format choices matter; MMLU gains concentrate on "=" questions. **[full text]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12183)

### Inferences
- The practical synthesis across Tam, dottxt, and Format Tax: if a joint call must return label + rationale/quote in a schema, field order matters (rationale/evidence before label preserves CoT benefits; label-first mimics direct answering). A decoupled design (free reasoning or label first, then a formatting/extraction pass) avoids the ordering confound at the cost of a second call.

### Gaps
- Format Tax numbers (per-model recovery percentages) not verified beyond abstract.
- The search surfaced a 2026 tool-schema study claiming a 43.5-point executable-accuracy drop under hard schema constraints (arXiv 2605.26128) but only via a third-party overview; not verified and not cited as a finding.
