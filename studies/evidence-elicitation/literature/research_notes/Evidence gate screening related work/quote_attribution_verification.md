## Key Question 1: Does any published work quantify decision-level loss (recall/coverage loss) caused by requiring verified verbatim evidence as an admission gate?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only in a handful of 2026 preprints; no peer-reviewed paper was found that frames "evidence-gated classification" as a named technique. The closest direct measurement (Windisch et al., medRxiv 2026) shows requiring a verbatim quote reduced coverage by ~2-6 points depending on model, and gating further on *semantic* support raised macro-F1 while lowering coverage further; the quote-existence check filters far fewer predictions than the quote-supports-label check. Older attribution work (FEVER, Bohnet et al., LongCite) quantifies the analogous drop when evidence is required for credit.

### Cited Findings
- Windisch et al. (oncology RCT eligibility-scope classification from 200 abstracts; GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Flash, Claude Opus 4.5; label-only vs label+verbatim quote; models could abstain; 3 repeats): coverage dropped from 86.2%→84.3% (GPT-5.2), 98.3%→92.8% (Gemini 3 Flash), 96.0%→94.5% (Claude Opus 4.5) when a quote was required; quotes were counted valid only if an exact substring of the abstract after whitespace normalization; mechanically valid quotes appeared in 83.3-91.2% of runs; only 48.0-78.8% of evidence-bearing predictions were judged (by a separate LLM judge) to be semantically supported by their quote; conditional macro-F1 rose slightly for GPT-5.2 and Gemini and fell for Claude; restricting to semantically supported predictions raised macro-F1 but lowered coverage; authors say the coverage/verifiability trade-off "depends on the model" and that the requirement "introduces new failure modes" (abstentions, invalid outputs, substring-valid but non-supporting quotes) — [Show Your Work, medRxiv 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1) [PARTIAL]
- Verifiable by Construction (clinical guideline QA, 12 models, citation marker `{{cite:section|quote}}`): a "claim funnel" reports, per model, % claims covered by a citation → % with a verbatim-compliant quote → % strictly supported. Weak models lose a lot at the verbatim gate (gpt-4.1-mini: 49.6% covered → 36.3% verbatim; claude-haiku-4.5: 74.8% → 56.5%; glm-4.7-flash: 66.1% → 55.2%) while strong models lose almost nothing (gpt-5-mini 100.0 → 99.1; claude-opus-5 98.0 → 98.0; deepseek-v4-pro 98.5 → 97.6). The much larger loss is at the *support* gate: Certified Claim Rate (verbatim AND fully supported) ranges 13.2% (gpt-4.1-mini) to 78.3% (gpt-5-mini), with claude-opus-5 at only 37.1% despite 98% verbatim compliance — [arXiv 2609.15964v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15964v2) [PARTIAL]
- Same paper: a "Supported Claim Rate" that lets a judge search the sections the model read for *some* verbatim span supporting the claim recovers most of the gap (e.g., claude-opus-5 37.1% CCR → 85.1% SCR; gpt-5-mini 78.3 → 92.8), i.e., many claims were supportable but the model picked a non-sufficient quote — [arXiv 2609.15964v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15964v2) [PARTIAL]
- FEVER (the canonical evidence-gated label metric): pipeline accuracy is 50.91% when evidence is ignored but 31.87% when the label is counted correct only if correct evidence is also returned (for Supported/Refuted; NEI scored on label alone) — [Thorne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05355) [PARTIAL]
- SciFact: VeriSci baseline in the open setting scores 46.5 F1 on Abstract Label+Rationale vs 39.5 F1 on the stricter Sentence Selection+Label; with oracle abstracts 72.7 vs 60.6 — the sentence-level rationale requirement costs ~7-12 F1 points — [Wadden et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.14974v6) [PARTIAL]
- LongCite: asking models to emit sentence-level citations in one pass via in-context learning lowers answer correctness (most tested models had a "correctness ratio" below 100% relative to citation-free answering); post-hoc citation keeps correctness at 100% but adds latency; training on citation-augmented data reversed the trade-off for LongCite-8B/9B — [arXiv 2409.02897v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02897v3) [PARTIAL]
- ReClaim (constrained sentence-level references): ~90.7 average Correct Attribution Score vs ALCE/ChatGPT, but with about a 6.0% drop in answer accuracy, attributed to relying on passage content rather than internal knowledge, and >2x output length — [arXiv 2407.01796v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01796v2) [PARTIAL]
- Attribute or Abstain (LAB, EMNLP 2024): "citation" (response + evidence in one step) performs best for large and fine-tuned models; extra retrieval helps small prompted models; evidence quality predicts response quality for simple responses but not complex ones "as models struggle with providing evidence for complex claims"; evidence F1 for fixed-vocabulary tasks is 1 if no evidence predicted when none annotated, otherwise 0 — [Buchmann et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.463/) [FULL]
- Bohnet et al. (Attributed QA): exact-match correlates only Pearson 0.45 with human AIS; retrieve-then-read human AIS 65.5 (71.4 with AutoAIS reranking) vs post-hoc retrieval 55.6 (59.0 reranked) vs LLM-as-retriever 46.0, so post-hoc attachment of evidence to an already-generated answer loses ~10 AIS points — [arXiv 2212.08037v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08037v2) [PARTIAL]
- Abstention vs Hallucination (REASONS): argues precision/recall "cannot distinguish systems that achieve high precision by abstaining from uncertain cases" and proposes Abstention Rate and Hallucination Rate; naive RAG HR 87.56% / AR 5.03%; advanced RAG HR 65.41% / AR 0%; documents "evidence-induced confidence": adding (even misleading) retrieved evidence drives abstention toward 0 — [arXiv 2405.02228v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02228) [PARTIAL]
- CiteGuard: an LLM-as-judge citation verifier (ChatGPT-4o) rejects many correct citations, with recall as low as 16-17%, attributed to lack of domain knowledge and terminology sensitivity; retrieval-augmented validation raises CiteME accuracy from 35.4% (CiteAgent+GPT-4o) to 45.1% (CiteGuard+GPT-4o) and 68.1% (CiteGuard+DeepSeek-R1, near 69.2% human) — [arXiv 2510.17853v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17853v4) [PARTIAL]
- Quote-Tuning side effect: TruthfulQA "informative" dropped 92.0%→89.5% while "truthful" rose 54.2%→61.8%; authors attribute the informativeness drop to a more conservative model that declines to answer more often — [arXiv 2404.03862v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03862v2) [PARTIAL]
- Searches for the literal phrases "evidence-gated", "cite-or-abstain", "attribution-constrained", "quote-verified", "grounded classification" returned no paper using them as a named technique — [WebSearch, Oct 2026]

### Inferences
- Across the two 2026 preprints that implement a quote gate, the *lexical* gate (quote exists) removes a small fraction of decisions for frontier models (0-6 pts) but a large fraction for small/cheap models (13-19 pts); the *semantic* gate (quote supports the label) is where most decisions are lost (20-60 pts), so a gate's recall cost is dominated by how "support" is adjudicated, not by string matching.
- The FEVER 50.9%→31.9% and SciFact 46.5→39.5 drops are older-model numbers but establish the pattern that "label correct" >> "label correct with correct evidence"; modern LLM funnels (CCR vs SCR) show the same structure.
- REASONS and Quote-Tuning both suggest that requiring evidence changes abstention behavior in both directions: evidence in context suppresses abstention (overconfidence), while quote-tuned models abstain more (lower informativeness).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that reports classification recall (TP/(TP+FN)) under a verbatim-evidence admission gate with and without the gate on the same labeled set; Windisch et al. report coverage and conditional macro-F1 rather than per-class recall, and it is a preprint.
- No work found that quantifies the gate's cost as a function of quote-length instruction (e.g., "short contiguous span" vs free) on the same task.
- Could not retrieve the full text of Windisch et al. (HTTP 429); the per-model quote-validity breakdown and the exact F1 deltas are therefore unverified.

## Key Question 2: What is known about lexical copy-failure modes (ellipses, merged fragments, paraphrase, normalization/Unicode) and the fuzzy-match tolerances used in practice?

### Takeaway
Exact-substring verification of LLM quotes typically passes 80-99% of quotes from frontier models and as low as ~42% for small models; the dominant non-fabricated failure modes are formatting/normalization differences, ellipsis-joined fragments, and faithful paraphrase. Practice has converged on tiered matching: exact → Unicode/whitespace/punctuation-normalized → ellipsis-aware ordered-fragment match → (optionally) LLM classification of the remainder into formatting/paraphrase/hallucination; published fuzzy thresholds (85-90 partial-ratio) come only from practitioner posts, not papers.

### Cited Findings
- VetScore (veterinary long-form QA with citations and excerpts, 6 LLMs): per-model exact-substring match of excerpts against cited source: Gemini 3.1 Pro 93.3%, Claude Opus 4.6 88.5%, Qwen 3.5 397B 84.4%, GPT-5 80.2%, DeepSeek V3.2 71.4%, Mistral Small 41.5%; "fuzzy" (ellipsis-aware, "..." joining any lines in source) match: 93.9 / 87.3 / 84.7 / 92.6 / 79.6 / 45.6; after an LLM classifier reassigns unmatched excerpts, "fuzzy+formatting" reaches 98.3 / 96.6 / 93.8 / 96.5 / 91.0 / 62.1 and "fuzzy+paraphrase" 99.8 / 99.7 / 98.3 / 99.3 / 98.1 / 85.5; non-existent source IDs were rare (max 2.1%, Mistral). GPT-5(.4) "tends to use ellipses frequently", producing the large exact-vs-fuzzy gap (80.2 vs 92.6) — [arXiv 2608.03675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03675) [FULL]
- VetScore unmatched-excerpt taxonomy: (1) formatting — found in source with only formatting differences; (2) paraphrase — faithful paraphrase of part of the source; (3) hallucination — excerpt or part fabricated. A Gemini 3.1 Pro classifier validated on 100 manually annotated cases reached 89% accuracy / macro-F1 (per-class F1: formatting 0.90, paraphrase 0.89, hallucination 0.86). Mistral Small paraphrased ~23% and fabricated >14% of excerpts — [arXiv 2608.03675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03675) [FULL]
- Verifiable by Construction tiers: Exact (character-for-character); Normalized (lowercase, Unicode canonical form, curly quotes/dashes folded to ASCII, whitespace collapsed, reference markers/bullets/table markup stripped); Elided (contains an ellipsis and fragments appear in order; fragments <10 chars ignored, at least two must remain). Tier breakdown: claude-haiku-4.5 44.8% exact + 23.9% normalized = 69.0% compliant; gpt-4.1-mini 55.8% exact + 7.3% elided = 66.2%; claude-opus-5 99.5% exact / 100.0% compliant; normalized-only matches are rare for most models — [arXiv 2609.15964v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15964v2) [PARTIAL]
- GRACE (Canadian statutes) citation tiers: verbatim (exact or whitespace-normalized); formatting-only differences (quotation marks, section notes, terminal punctuation removed); assembled (non-contiguous fragments each occurring verbatim); partial (at least half of fragments found); unsupported (none). Questions with partial/unsupported citations were removed from the dataset. Evaluation reports verbatim / grounded / unsupported rates: CLeAR-4B open-book 68.9% / 80.2% / 19.5%; Qwen3-4B open-book 66.7% / 78.9% / 21.1%; closed-book (statute withheld) unsupported rises to 83.2% and 96.7% respectively — [arXiv 2609.23726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23726) [FULL]
- ResearchQA: plain substring matching rejected as brittle because PyPDF2 extraction adds "line-break hyphenation, mid-word spaces, ligatures, smart quotes"; final method is a normalization pipeline applied to both quote and source with a whitespace-stripped fallback to recover citations broken by mid-word PDF artifacts; embedding similarity rejected because it "would pass a fabricated-but-on-topic quote"; no fuzzy threshold used — [arXiv 2607.11074v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11074v1) [PARTIAL]
- Quote-Tuning measures quoting with exact character 25-gram membership in a Bloom-filter Data Portrait of Wikipedia (Pile subset); the authors call exact-match quoting "a lower bound due to potential whitespace mismatches" — [arXiv 2404.03862v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03862v2) [PARTIAL]
- According-to prompting's QUIP-Score is character n-gram overlap with the pretraining corpus; sanity check: average QUIP of Wikipedia documents is 99.9% ± 0.1 vs 17.0% ± 0.8 on the Pile — [arXiv 2305.13252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13252) [SEARCH]
- Verbatim copying collapses with length: in a numeric-constant transcription task (exact substring check per number; rounded/scientific rewrites count as missing), mean match rate fell from 63.46% (N=100) to 15.65% (N=300) to 7.12% (N=500); no model achieved a perfect run at N≥300; failure modes were capacity-limited truncation (prefix of list then stop) and derailment; the validator "measures verbatim inclusion rather than semantic numeric equivalence" — [arXiv 2601.03640v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03640v1) [PARTIAL]
- Windisch et al.: validity = exact substring after whitespace normalization; 83.3-91.2% of runs produced a mechanically valid quote — [medRxiv 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1) [PARTIAL]
- ERASER (on exact-span scoring of rationales): "exact matches between predicted and reference rationales is likely too harsh" (footnote: "an extra token destroys the match but not usually the meaning"), motivating token-level IOU with a 0.5 overlap threshold counted as a match — [DeYoung et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03429) [FULL]
- Practitioner thresholds (non-peer-reviewed): rapidfuzz partial-ratio cutoff 85 with a deterministic validator — [Medium post](https://medium.com/@taotang757/my-ai-kept-hallucinating-citations-heres-the-code-that-fixed-it-353d0dbc0d78); ~90% best-match with a warning that partial-ratio lets short fragments score 100 inside longer text — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/experilearning/unlocking-advanced-rag-citations-and-attributions-59lk). A bibliographic-title matcher found 80% too permissive (21% hallucinated titles passed), 95% lost recall on formatting variants, settled on 90% — [arXiv 2606.21359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21359) [SEARCH; applies to reference titles, not quotes]
- Anthropic's API-level approach removes the copy step entirely: "the API parses citations into the response formats ... and extracts `cited_text` directly, citations are guaranteed to contain valid pointers to the provided documents"; `cited_text` does not count toward output tokens — [Anthropic Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) [FULL]

### Inferences
- The consistent ordering of failure causes in both VetScore and Verifiable-by-Construction is: formatting/normalization > ellipsis/assembly > paraphrase > outright fabrication, with fabrication concentrated in small models; a normalizer plus ellipsis-aware ordered-fragment matcher recovers most "false invalid" quotes without admitting fabricated ones.
- Ellipsis usage is model-specific (GPT-5 family in VetScore; gpt-4.1-mini "elided" tier), so a gate that forbids ellipses will differentially penalize certain models unless the instruction specifies a single contiguous span.
- No paper reports a ratio-based fuzzy threshold; the research-grade pipelines use deterministic normalization tiers precisely to avoid the fabricated-but-on-topic leak that embedding/fuzzy scores permit.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that varies the quoting instruction ("short contiguous span", "no ellipses", max-length) and measures the change in exact-match rate on the same task.
- No paper reports Unicode-specific failure counts (e.g., NBSP, ligatures, curly quotes) as a separate category; they are folded into "normalized"/"formatting".
- Published fuzzy-ratio thresholds are only in blog posts; none are validated against labeled quote data in a paper I could verify.

## Key Question 3: What methods replace generation with selection (sentence IDs, span offsets, constrained decoding) and what accuracy/cost trade-offs are reported?

### Takeaway
Three selection mechanisms are documented: (a) sentence-index citation after deterministic sentence segmentation (LongCite, SelfCite), (b) trie-constrained decoding so the model can only emit exact source sentences (ReClaim), and (c) API-side structured citations that return char/page/block offsets and extracted `cited_text` (Anthropic). Reported gains are large on citation accuracy (ReClaim ~90 CAS; LongCite +3.6-6.4 F1 over GPT-4o with ~2x finer granularity) at the cost of a small answer-accuracy drop when prompted (ReClaim -6%; LongCite CR<100% for ICL) and longer outputs/latency; selection guarantees pointer validity but not semantic support.

### Cited Findings
- LongCite: source split into NLTK sentences, numbered; each statement cites `[k]` or `[a-b]`; chunk-level (128-token) citations rejected because they "often cut sentences mid-way"; LongBench-Cite metrics: citation recall per statement scored 0/0.5/1 (full/partial/no support, GPT-4o judge; statements needing no citation count as recalled), precision = cited snippet supports some key point, F1, plus citation length (avg tokens of cited snippets; shorter = finer). LongCite-8B/9B citation F1 72.0/69.2 vs GPT-4o 65.6; citation length 85/91 tokens vs 220 for GPT-4o; correctness 71.7/70.4 vs 69.4; training on citation data raised correctness ~16%/28% over plain long-context SFT — [arXiv 2409.02897v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02897v3) [PARTIAL]
- SelfCite: self-supervised reward via context ablation (necessity: removing cited text should change the response; sufficiency: keeping only cited text should preserve it); used for best-of-N and preference optimization; +5.3 citation F1 on LongBench-Cite; ICML 2025 — [arXiv 2502.09604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09604) [SEARCH]
- ReClaim: passages split into sentences and encoded as a prefix tree; reference-generation token selection restricted to tree paths so every reference is an exact source sentence; interleaves reference then claim; metrics Correct Attribution Score (answer entirely supported by cited sentences) and Citation Redundancy Score; avg CAS 90.7 (Llama3-8B w/IG: 92.1 ASQA, 89.9 ELI5, 90.1 ExpertQA); vs ALCE/ChatGPT +31.3% CAS, +16.7% CRS, +25.7% MAUVE, but ~6.0% lower answer accuracy; citations ~22% the length of ALCE's; responses more than double in length; weak on multi-source synthesis/multi-hop — [arXiv 2407.01796v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01796v2) [PARTIAL]
- FRONT (quote-then-answer): stage 1 fine-tunes the model to output grounding quotes (document ID + extracted segment) before the answer; stage 2 DPO for quote-answer consistency; ALCE citation F1: ASQA Front-7B 73.59 / Front-13B 75.97 vs ChatGPT 71.22; ELI5 56.92/59.75 vs 48.22; QAMPARI 24.79 vs 20.44. Quote authenticity is checked only by human binary annotation (0.94 ASQA, 0.92 ELI5, 0.86 QAMPARI; ChatGPT 0.93; Fleiss κ 0.82) — no automated exact-match check; citation quality uses TRUE NLI, not quote matching — [arXiv 2408.04568](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04568) [PARTIAL]
- Anthropic Citations API: documents are chunked to define minimum citation granularity — plain text and PDF are chunked into sentences; custom-content documents use user-supplied blocks with no further chunking ("for bullet points or transcripts"); citation objects: `char_location` {cited_text, document_index, document_title, start_char_index (0-indexed), end_char_index (exclusive)}, `page_location` {start_page_number (1-indexed), end_page_number (exclusive)}, `content_block_location` {start_block_index (0-indexed), end_block_index (exclusive)}; "the model outputs citations in a standardized format that are then parsed into cited text and document location indices"; `cited_text` not counted toward output or (when passed back) input tokens; "In Anthropic's evaluations, the citations feature is significantly more likely to cite the most relevant quotes from documents than purely prompt-based approaches" (no numbers given); scanned PDFs without extractable text are not citable — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations) [FULL]
- Bohnet et al.: attribution unit `c` is drawn from a fixed set 𝒞 (e.g., paragraphs in a corpus) — selection, not free text — and AutoAIS (T5-11B NLI, premise = attribution, hypothesis = question+answer, threshold 0.5) correlates 0.96 Pearson with human AIS at system level but much lower at instance level — [arXiv 2212.08037v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08037v2) [PARTIAL]
- Attribute or Abstain: evidence = segments d (paragraphs) of document D; five configurations (post-hoc, retrieve-then-read, citation, reduced-post-hoc, reduced-citation); "citation" (one-step response + evidence IDs) is best for large/fine-tuned models — [Buchmann et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.463/) [FULL]
- ERASER baselines include "pipeline" models that first extract rationales (sentence-level, GRU encoder) and then predict using only the rationales, with no shared parameters — the classical selection-then-decide architecture — [DeYoung et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03429) [FULL]
- Survey of 134 papers: among text-based studies, cited evidence is document-level in 43%, paragraph-level 40%, sentence-level 12%, token-level 2%; the survey does not cover copy/constrained-decoding/pointer methods or quote matching — [arXiv 2508.15396v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15396v1) [PARTIAL]
- FACTS Grounding judge prompt experiments: for Claude 3.5 Sonnet the "implicit span-level" template (sentence-by-sentence labeling with supporting excerpt) gave the best macro-F1 (70.24) vs response-level (61.88); for Gemini 1.5 Pro a JSON template was best (71.47); judges are asked to "Provide a supporting excerpt from the context" for each sentence — [arXiv 2501.03200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03200) [FULL]
- General constrained-decoding searches found no paper applying grammar/regex-constrained decoding specifically to citation-index selection other than ReClaim's trie — [WebSearch, Oct 2026]

### Inferences
- Selection mechanisms eliminate the "quote not in source" failure class entirely (pointer validity is guaranteed by construction) but shift the burden to the "quote supports claim" check, which all of these systems still evaluate with an NLI model or LLM judge.
- Sentence-ID citation depends on a deterministic segmenter (NLTK in LongCite, sentence chunking in Anthropic's API); mismatched segmentation between producer and verifier is a latent failure mode not quantified in any source found.
- Costs reported are output length (ReClaim >2x), a modest prompted-answer accuracy drop (ReClaim ~6%; LongCite CR<100%), and training requirements to recover it (LongCite-45k SFT, FRONT DPO).

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports latency/cost of trie-constrained decoding numerically; ReClaim only states outputs are longer.
- Anthropic's claim of better citation quality vs prompting is stated without published numbers.
- SelfCite details come from search summaries only; its exact sentence-ID format was not verified.

## Key Question 4: How do attribution papers separate "quote is in source" from "quote supports the claim", and what human annotation protocols do they use?

### Takeaway
The field treats these as two different checks: lexical presence (string/pointer validity; largely automated) and attribution/support (AIS "according to P, s"; NLI-automated with human gold). Human protocols are two-stage (interpretability → attribution; AIS), three-way (attributable / extrapolatory / contradictory; AttrScore) or graded (full / partial / no support; ALCE, LongCite, Verifiable-by-Construction), with reported agreement from Krippendorff α ≈ 0.5-0.9 and Cohen κ ≈ 0.7-0.85. The extractive-rationale literature adds a third axis — faithfulness (sufficiency/comprehensiveness) vs plausibility — that is orthogonal to both.

### Cited Findings
- AIS definition: an utterance s is attributable to parts P of corpus K iff "A generic hearer will, with a chosen level of confidence, affirm the following statement: 'According to P, s'"; P may be "pointers to paragraphs or sentences within K"; attribution is judged on explicatures (decontextualized meaning) — [Rashkin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12870) [FULL]
- AIS two-stage protocol: stage 1 shows output + prior context without the source and asks a yes/no interpretability question ("Is all of the information relayed by the system summary interpretable to you?"); stage 2 asks yes/no attribution against the source; annotators are told relevance is irrelevant to attribution ("Partially and fully relevant responses can be equally assessed for attribution"); whole outputs are rated because "if even one span within the model output is not AIS, then the whole output is not AIS"; authors note yes/no questions reduce complexity but "it may be useful to have more fine-grained measures" — [Rashkin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12870) [FULL]
- AIS agreement (crowd, Krippendorff α on AIS ratings): QReCC 0.76, WoW 0.79, ToTTo 0.74, CNN/DM 0.69 (interpretability α: QReCC 0.91, WoW 0.60, ToTTo 0.84, CNN/DM 0.46); vs expert on AIS: QReCC α 0.54, CNN/DM 0.61 — [Rashkin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12870) [FULL]
- AIS explicitly avoids factuality: "rather than making factuality judgments" it evaluates support relative to a given source; contrasted with FEVER-style fact verification (Thorne et al. 2021) — [Rashkin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12870) [FULL]
- AttrScore label set: attributable (reference fully supports statement), extrapolatory (reference lacks sufficient information), contradictory (statement directly contradicts reference); AttrEval-GenSearch = ~250 New Bing outputs across 12 domains annotated by the authors; AttrEval-Simulation repurposed from QA/fact-checking/NLI/summarization; zero-shot GPT-4 overall 85.1 on GenSearch (attributable 87.3, contradictory 45.0, extrapolatory 89.6) but 55.6 on Simulation; major failures: "insensitivity to fine-grained information comparisons, such as overlooking contextual cues in the reference, disregard for numerical values, and failure in performing symbolic operations" — [Yue et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06311) [FULL]
- ALCE: citation recall per statement = 1 iff ≥1 citation and NLI(concat(cited passages) ⊨ statement) with TRUE (T5-11B); precision = each citation must (a) be part of a recall-1 statement and (b) not be removable without losing entailment; "this algorithm overlooks the scenario when one citation partially supports the statement"; human protocol asks whether a citation "fully supports", "partially supports", or "does not support" the sentence, with precision credit for at least partial support; Cohen κ human-vs-ALCE 0.698 (recall), 0.525 (precision); accuracy vs human gold 85.1% / 77.6%; on ELI5 ~50% of ChatGPT/GPT-4 generations are not fully supported by cited passages; shortcut analysis: copying the top-1 passage verbatim and citing itself gets near-perfect citation scores but low fluency/correctness — [Gao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627) [FULL]
- ALCE closed-book finding: models "often generate texts that are correct but not similar to any retrieved passages, making it difficult to match a citation post-hoc" (CLOSEDBOOK ELI5 citation recall 15.5 vs 51.1 for VANILLA ChatGPT) — [Gao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627) [FULL]
- Verifiable by Construction separates Verbatim Compliance (string tiers) from Claim Support (LLM judge sees question, claim, quotes only; labels fully/partially/not supported/contradicted; strict = fully, lenient = fully or partially); partial-support failure types: unsupported addition (most common), strength inflation, scope inflation, causal upgrade; human check: 2 author-annotators on 70 stratified claim/quote pairs, κ=0.853 between annotators, κ 0.858 / 0.810 vs judge; judge verdicts are majority of 3 samples; lenient support is 92-100% but strict support only 33.8-79.0% among cited claims — [arXiv 2609.15964v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15964v2) [PARTIAL]
- GRACE states the separation directly: "String matching verifies that a quotation is real but not whether the reasoning based on it is sound," then applies an LLM judge (GLM-5.2) scoring grounding and other dimensions against the full act — [arXiv 2609.23726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23726) [FULL]
- VetScore: after lexical matching, veterinary experts annotate claim-level fact verification and harm potential; the lexical step classifies only formatting/paraphrase/hallucination of excerpts — [arXiv 2608.03675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03675) [FULL]
- FACTS Grounding: response labeled "accurate" only if all information-bearing claims are grounded in the context; three judges (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) chosen by macro-F1 against N=402 human-annotated held-out set; eligibility (fulfils request) judged first, ineligible responses disqualified even if fully grounded; final score averages judges — [arXiv 2501.03200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03200) [FULL]
- ERASER faithfulness: comprehensiveness = m(x)_j − m(x \ r)_j (prediction change when rationale removed); sufficiency = m(x)_j − m(r)_j (change when only rationale kept); AOPC variants over top 1/5/10/20/50% tokens; plausibility = agreement with human rationales (token-level IOU F1, threshold 0.5, and token P/R/F1); the paper notes "a model may provide rationales that are plausible (agreeable to humans) but that it did not rely on for its output" — [DeYoung et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03429) [FULL]
- Jacovi & Goldberg: faithfulness should be "defined and evaluated explicitly, and independently from plausibility"; "a faithful interpretation is one that accurately represents the reasoning process behind the model's prediction"; they "call for discarding the binary notion of faithfulness in favor of a more graded one" — [Jacovi & Goldberg 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03685) [FULL]
- FEVER: labels Supported / Refuted / NotEnoughInfo; NEI when a claim "could not be supported or refuted by any amount of information in Wikipedia"; NEI claims have no gold evidence (NEI training instances are simulated by sampling sentences); Supported/Refuted count correct only if correct evidence returned, all sentences of a multi-sentence set required, one complete set suffices; 5-way Fleiss κ 0.6841; evidence precision 95.42% / recall 72.36% vs super-annotators — [Thorne et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.05355v3) [PARTIAL]
- SciFact: Supports / Refutes / NoInfo; rationale = minimal sentence set a domain expert could judge as implying the claim, ≤3 sentences, ≤3 rationales per abstract, mutually exclusive; sentence-level credit requires the sentence to belong to a gold rationale AND all other sentences of that rationale be predicted AND the abstract not predicted NoInfo; abstract-level requires predicted rationales to contain some gold rationale; NoInfo pairs = 516 of 1,409 claims; label κ 0.75, rationale κ 0.71 — [Wadden et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.14974v6) [PARTIAL]
- Climate-FEVER: 1,535 claims × top-5 Wikipedia evidence sentences = 7,675 pairs; per-pair SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NOT_ENOUGH_INFO by majority with NEI on tie; claim-level default NEI, SUPPORTS/REFUTES if evidence of that type, DISPUTED if both; Krippendorff α 0.334 (authors: hard even for humans); claim distribution SUPPORTS 42.67%, REFUTES 16.5%, DISPUTED 9.97%, NEI 30.88% (paper's Table 4 lists the SUPPORTS/NEI counts in opposite order — internal inconsistency); FEVER-trained baseline 38.78% accuracy vs ~77.7% on FEVER dev — [Diggelmann et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.00614v2) [PARTIAL]
- HealthVer: 14,330 evidence-claim pairs, labels Support / Refute / Neutral, claims from search snippets, evidence from scientific abstracts re-ranked with T5; abstract gives no agreement figures — [Sarrouti et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.297/) [ABS]
- REASONS human check: 1,000 outputs, r=0.89 with automated hallucination rate, κ=0.78 inter-annotator; factual errors outnumbered acceptable paraphrases ~12.7:1 — [arXiv 2405.02228v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02228) [PARTIAL]

### Inferences
- The strongest empirical demonstration that lexical validity ≠ support is the Verifiable-by-Construction funnel: claude-opus-5 has 100% verbatim compliance but only ~37% strict support, and lenient support (92-100%) vs strict (34-79%) shows that most "unsupported" quotes are partial support (added content, scope/strength inflation), not fabrication.
- FEVER-family datasets handle "not enough info" by (a) scoring NEI on label only with no evidence requirement (FEVER, SciFact), and (b) making NEI the default when evidence is insufficient or contradictory (Climate-FEVER adds DISPUTED). This maps directly onto an evidence-gate design: an abstain/NEI outcome needs no quote, and the gate should distinguish "no evidence found" from "evidence found but insufficient".
- Agreement on binary attribution is high (κ ≈ 0.7-0.85) when claims are short and decontextualized; it drops for long outputs (AIS CNN/DM α 0.46 interpretability) and for noisy real-world evidence (Climate-FEVER α 0.33), so support judgments on retrieved real-world snippets are inherently noisier than on curated passages.

### Gaps
- No source found that reports inter-annotator agreement specifically on the question "does this verbatim quote (as opposed to a passage) support this label" except Verifiable by Construction's 70-pair author annotation.
- HealthVer's definition of "neutral" and its agreement statistics were not retrievable from the landing page.
- According-to prompting and SelfCite were not opened beyond search summaries; their human-evaluation protocols are unverified here.
