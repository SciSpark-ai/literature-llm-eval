## Key Question 1: Standard evaluation frameworks for a policy that routes some items to human review (risk-coverage, cost-sensitive deferral, workload-matched comparison); metrics and statistical units

### Takeaway
Three evaluation traditions coexist and are rarely combined: (a) selective classification (risk-coverage curves, AURC/AUARC, coverage@risk) from the deep-learning literature; (b) learning-to-defer / human-AI complementarity (system accuracy with expert cost, calibrated P(expert correct), deferral rate, capacity/congestion constraints); and (c) the systematic-review-screening tradition (recall / "lost evidence", WSS@R, workload reduction counted in manual classifications or reviewer-hours, usually with the review as the statistical unit). In LLM screening work the dominant practical design is "accept when models agree / defer disagreements to a human" with workload reported as fraction of records (or classifications) still needing human labels; recall-vs-review-fraction curves are almost never reported, and false-negative cost is almost never priced into workload claims.

### Cited Findings

**Selective classification foundations (abstract-only unless noted)**
- Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017), "Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks" (NeurIPS 2017; arXiv 1705.08500): constructs a selective classifier (f, g) from a trained network; the user sets a target risk and the method rejects inputs so that risk is met "with high probability"; headline: 2% top-5 ImageNet error guaranteed with probability 99.9% at almost 60% coverage. The paper defines selective risk as the loss on accepted points divided by coverage (expected acceptance rate) and plots the risk-coverage (RC) curve. — [arXiv 1705.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500) (abstract page read; RC-curve definitions are from the full paper and not re-verified here).
- Geifman & El-Yaniv (2019), "SelectiveNet" (ICML 2019; arXiv 1901.09192): trains classification and rejection end-to-end and reports "consistently improved risk-coverage trade-off". — [arXiv 1901.09192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09192)
- Geifman, Uziel & El-Yaniv (2019), "Bias-Reduced Uncertainty Estimation for Deep Neural Classifiers" (ICLR 2019; arXiv 1805.08206): the paper that popularised AURC (area under the risk-coverage curve) and E-AURC as summary metrics for ranking confidence scores. — [arXiv 1805.08206](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08206) (abstract only; AURC definition not re-verified from body text)
- Jaeger et al. (2022), "A Call to Reflect on Evaluation Practices for Failure Detection in Image Classification" (ICLR 2023; arXiv 2211.15259): argues that OOD detection, uncertainty estimation, and confidence scoring all target the same goal (detecting classifier failures) but use incompatible protocols, and proposes AURC as the unifying metric. — [arXiv 2211.15259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15259) (abstract only)
- Kamath, Jia & Liang (2020), "Selective Question Answering under Domain Shift" (ACL 2020; arXiv 2006.09462): sets the "answer as many as possible while maintaining high accuracy" framing for NLP; shows softmax-probability abstention "fare[s] poorly" under shift because models are overconfident OOD, and trains a separate calibrator. — [arXiv 2006.09462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09462) (abstract only)
- Ren et al. (2023), "Out-of-Distribution Detection and Selective Generation for Conditional Language Models" (ICLR 2023; arXiv 2209.15558): extends selective prediction to generation ("selective generation"), abstaining on low-quality outputs under distribution shift. — [arXiv 2209.15558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15558) (abstract only)

**Abstention-metric taxonomy for LLMs (read beyond abstract)**
- Wen et al. (2024), "Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in Large Language Models" (TACL 2024; arXiv 2407.18418) lists the metric zoo: abstention accuracy, abstention precision/recall/F1 ("Prudence Score"), URUP and ARSP (unsafe-on-unsafe, abstained-on-safe), coverage/acceptance rate, abstention rate, benign answering rate, R-Acc (reliable accuracy = accuracy among answered), Effective Reliability (ER), Abstain-ECE, Coverage@Acc, AURCC (area under risk-coverage; lower better), AUACC (area under accuracy-coverage; higher better), and AUROC for separating correct from incorrect. Methods are organised by LLM lifecycle (pretraining, alignment, inference: input-processing / in-processing [probing, uncertainty estimation, calibration, consistency, prompting] / output-processing [self-evaluation, LLM collaboration]). Retrieval-/evidence-/verification-based abstention is not a named category. — [arXiv 2407.18418 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18418)

**Learning to defer / human-AI complementarity (abstract-only unless noted)**
- Madras, Pitassi & Zemel (2018), "Predict Responsibly: Improving Fairness and Accuracy by Learning to Defer" (NeurIPS 2018; arXiv 1711.06664): introduces "learning to defer" as a generalisation of rejection learning that models the downstream decision-maker's behaviour and biases rather than treating "Pass" as a fixed cost. — [arXiv 1711.06664](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06664)
- Mozannar & Sontag (2020), "Consistent Estimators for Learning to Defer to an Expert" (ICML 2020; arXiv 2006.01862): classifier + rejector trained jointly from samples of expert decisions; reduction to cost-sensitive learning with a consistent surrogate loss generalising cross-entropy. — [arXiv 2006.01862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01862)
- Verma & Nalisnick (2022), "Calibrated Learning to Defer with One-vs-All Classifiers" (ICML 2022; arXiv 2202.03673): shows Mozannar & Sontag's multiclass surrogate is not calibrated w.r.t. expert correctness and can produce invalid probabilities; proposes one-vs-all parameterisation yielding calibrated P(expert correct). Relevant because routing thresholds need calibrated P(human correct | x). — [arXiv 2202.03673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03673)
- Raghu et al. (2019), "The Algorithmic Automation Problem: Prediction, Triage, and Human Effort" (arXiv 1903.12220): frames automation as choosing which instances to give the algorithm (triage) as an optimisation problem, separate from head-to-head accuracy; shows simple heuristics can be near-optimal. — [arXiv 1903.12220](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12220)
- Wilder, Horvitz & Kamar (2020), "Learning to Complement Humans" (IJCAI 2020; arXiv 2005.00582): end-to-end optimisation of team performance; "focus machine learning on problem instances that are difficult for humans, while recognizing instances that are difficult for the machine and seeking human input". — [arXiv 2005.00582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00582)
- Bansal et al. (2021), "Is the Most Accurate AI the Best Teammate? Optimizing AI for Teamwork" (AAAI 2021; arXiv 2004.13102): argues predictable performance may be worth a slight accuracy sacrifice when a human makes final decisions. — [arXiv 2004.13102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13102)
- Bansal et al. (2021), "Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance" (CHI 2021; arXiv 2006.14779): with an AI of human-comparable accuracy, explanations did not increase complementary performance beyond showing confidence; explanations increased acceptance of recommendations regardless of correctness. — [arXiv 2006.14779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779)
- Leitao et al. (2022), "Human-AI Collaboration in Decision-Making: Beyond Learning to Defer" (ICML 2022 HMCaT workshop; arXiv 2206.13202): critiques L2D's "often unfeasible requirements" (human predictions for every instance; ground-truth independent of the humans) and its silence on capacity management and dynamic environments. — [arXiv 2206.13202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13202)
- Lykouris & Weng (2024), "Learning to Defer in Congested Systems: The AI-Human Interplay" (arXiv 2402.12237): content-moderation pipeline with separate classification (auto-delete) and admission (send-to-human) thresholds; argues fixed thresholds ignore estimation uncertainty, time-varying arrivals, human review capacity, and selective-sampling feedback. — [arXiv 2402.12237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12237)
- Alves, Leitao et al. (2025), "A benchmarking framework and dataset for learning to defer in human-AI decision-making" (Scientific Data 12:506; PMC12019285): FiFAR dataset with predictions from 50 fraud analysts on 30K instances for multi-expert L2D benchmarking. — [DOI 10.1038/s41597-025-04664-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04664-y) (abstract via Europe PMC)
- Tailor et al. (2024), "Learning to Defer to a Population: A Meta-Learning Approach" (AISTATS 2024; arXiv 2403.02683); Mao, Mohri & Zhong (2023) multi-expert surrogate losses (arXiv 2310.14774); Bary, Macq & Petit (2025) "No Need for Learning to Defer? A Training Free Deferral Framework ... through Conformal Prediction" (arXiv 2509.12573); Paat & Shen (2025) "Conformal Set-based Human-AI Complementarity with Multiple Experts" (AAMAS 2025; arXiv 2508.06997); De Toni et al. (2024) "Towards Human-AI Complementarity with Prediction Sets" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2405.17544; conformal sets are suboptimal for human accuracy, optimal sets NP-hard); Cresswell et al. (2024) "Conformal Prediction Sets Improve Human Decision Making" (ICML 2024; arXiv 2401.13744; pre-registered RCT). — abstracts verified via arXiv API: [2403.02683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02683), [2310.14774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14774), [2509.12573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12573), [2508.06997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06997), [2405.17544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17544), [2401.13744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13744)

**Cost-sensitive deferral with explicit review cost (read beyond abstract)**
- Singh, Srikantha & Lakhanpal (2026, preprint), "Cost-Sensitive Conformal Prediction and Human-in-the-Loop Abstention for Imbalanced High-Stakes Decision Support" (arXiv 2607.27143): Mondrian (class-conditional) conformal prediction; deferral rule = predict only when the set is exactly {0} or {1}, defer when {0,1} or empty; expected cost = misclassification terms + abstention_rate x C_rev, with noisy-expert correction; derives a break-even review cost C_rev* below which deferral beats threshold tuning (C_rev* rises from 0.82 to 4.35 as C_FN goes 5 to 50; deferral still pays if expert error < 0.18). 15 OpenML tabular datasets, 7 classifiers, 10 seeds (3,150 runs); at C_rev = 0.5, cost 0.313 vs 0.682 (default) and 0.510 (Bayes-tuned threshold). Caveat: average abstention rate 71.9%; no LLM experiments. — [arXiv 2607.27143 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27143)
- Presacan et al. (2026), "When silence is safer: a review and decision-theoretic framework for LLM abstention in healthcare" (npj Digital Medicine 9:749; PMC13631354): formalises answer/abstain as expected utility E[U] = E[a(x) u_ans(y_hat, y) + (1 - a(x)) u_abs(x)] with task loss, harm function h, harm weight lambda_harm, abstention cost c_abs(x), and a scoring function s(x) thresholded at tau; recommends risk-coverage and "AOC" curves stratified by scenario type so under- and over-refusal are visible together; pilot on eight scenarios reports AURC 0.093 (MedSAFE) vs 0.163 (no-abstention baseline), AUAOC 0.929. — [DOI 10.1038/s41746-026-02882-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02882-1); full text via [Europe PMC XML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13631354/fullTextXML) (first ~100k chars read; Table 5 not seen)

**Conformal routing for LLM classification (read beyond abstract where noted)**
- Uddin & Bauer (2026), "Conformal LLM Routing with Distribution-Free Safety Guarantees" (ACL 2026 SRW, pp. 791-799): a logistic-regression gate on embeddings predicts per-query safety; Clopper-Pearson conformal calibration sets the routing threshold so the violation rate among routed queries stays below alpha with probability >= 1 - delta. Routing is between models (Mixtral-8x7B vs GPT-4, 24.5x cost gap), not to humans; GSM8K and MMLU. — [ACL Anthology 2026.acl-srw.70](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-srw.70/)
- Machcha et al. (2026), "Knowing When to Abstain: Medical LLMs Under Clinical Uncertainty" (EACL 2026 main; arXiv 2601.12471): MedAbstain uses LAC and APS conformal scores at alpha = 0.1 (30/70 calibration/test split) with set size as the uncertainty measure; explicit abstain option inserted as an extra MCQ choice; metrics = accuracy, set size, abstention rate; human evaluation on 50 items found model abstention precision 71.43% but recall 13.16%. No risk-coverage curve reported. — [arXiv 2601.12471 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12471)
- A scoping review of conformal prediction methods for LLMs exists (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A, DOI 10.1098/rsta.2026.0061; companion repo github.com/aliceinlatentspace/conformal-llm-review). Publisher page returned 403 and no arXiv mirror was found; NOT verified beyond the search snippet. — [DOI 10.1098/rsta.2026.0061](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2026.0061)

**Systematic-review-screening evaluation conventions (read beyond abstract)**
- Madeyski, Kitchenham & Shepperd (2025), "LLM4SCREENLIT: Recommendations on Assessing the Performance of LLMs for Screening Literature in Systematic Reviews" (arXiv 2511.12635 v2). Key recommendations: report Lost Evidence (1 - Recall) as primary, MCC, and a Weighted MCC (positives weighted w, default 10) with a justified FN:FP cost ratio (R1); base comparisons on cost-sensitive analysis (R2); predefine an acceptable Lost-Evidence threshold for deployment (R3); "Publish complete confusion matrices for every model, dataset, and prompt" (R4); unclassifiable/"unsure" outputs must not be dropped and should be counted as positives requiring human review; for a retrospective benchmark on the full labelled population the within-review standard error is effectively zero, so CIs are meaningful only under subsampling or pooling across reviews, and the review is the unit for cross-review synthesis; do not use binomial CIs or bootstrap on item-level data. Survey of 29 papers: ~10% reported MCC, ~24% full confusion matrices, "None of the five papers claiming workload savings priced false negatives." — [arXiv 2511.12635 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12635)
- Rahgozar & Mortezaagha (2026, preprint) define WSS@95 as "the fraction of records the reviewer can skip (relative to random screening)" at 95% recall, WSS@R = (TN + FN)/N - (1 - R); cost is token usage as a multiple of baseline; statistical unit = dataset (n = 8) with paired Wilcoxon tests, +/- are SDs across dataset means, no CIs. — [arXiv 2608.14551 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14551)
- Sanghera et al. (2025, JAMIA 32(5):893-904) count workload reduction as "the number of correctly excluded articles per 100 screened records" (the share of records humans need not appraise); series vs parallel ensembles trade precision for sensitivity. — [JAMIA doi:10.1093/jamia/ocaf050](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/893/8090299)
- Hilkenmeier, Stoltenberg & Stierle (2026, Systematic Reviews 15:191) count workload as manual classifications vs dual human screening: traditional 2,514 x 2 = 5,028 classifications; proposed workflow 1,458 (471 x 2 calibration + 396 x 1 non-agreement + 120 adjudications), i.e., >70% reduction; prespecified metrics Krippendorff's alpha and F1 with stop criterion alpha < 0.8 or F1 < 0.9 on a human-screened calibration sample. — [PMC13263951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263951/)
- Delgado-Chaves et al. (2025, PNAS 122(2):e2411962122) define one-reviewer workload reduction as records on which the first human reviewer and the LLM agree, over the original number of identified records; disagreements, unclassifiable, and preprocessing-dropped records go to a second human; range 33-93% across 18 models and three reviews. — [PMC11745399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11745399/)
- Figalova, Huestegge & Bockler-Raettig (2026, preprint; preregistered on OSF) report "retained workload" (number/percent of 1,131 records classified Include or Unclear) against "operational recall" over 316 verified-eligible records, plus logged human minutes (single reviewer 1.04 min/record; distributed team 1.44 min/record summed person-time). — [arXiv 2608.26885 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26885)
- Active-learning stopping rules (abstract only): Callaghan & Muller-Hansen (2020, Systematic Reviews 9:273) propose statistical stopping criteria that reject the hypothesis that a reviewer-set recall target has been missed at a chosen confidence level, with an average 17% work reduction on test sets. — [PMC7700715](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7700715/). Boetje & van de Schoot (2024, Systematic Reviews 13:81) propose the SAFE conservative multi-heuristic stopping procedure for ASReview; no recall target or workload numbers in abstract. — [PMC10908130](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10908130/)

**Human-AI hybrid evaluated by workload fraction (read beyond abstract)**
- Jain et al. (2025, DeepMind), "Human-AI Complementarity: A Goal for Amplified Oversight" (arXiv 2510.26518; also ACM DOI 10.1145/3805689.3812308): fact-verification of 1,918 Gemini sentences; AI confidence = fraction of 50 samples agreeing with the majority; items with confidence <= T = 0.62 routed to humans (280/1,918 = 14.6%); accuracy AI-only 87.7%, human individual 75.1%, human majority 80.6%, hybrid 89.3% (unassisted humans) and 91.3% (evidence-assisted humans); on the routed slice AI 60.5% vs human 71.3%. T was chosen on the evaluation set (no held-out tuning). — [arXiv 2510.26518 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26518v2)

### Inferences
- There is no single accepted framework; a defensible evaluation for an LLM screening gate should report (i) recall / lost evidence at each operating point, (ii) the human-review fraction (or classifications) as the x-axis, i.e., a recall-vs-review-fraction or cost-coverage curve, and (iii) a cost-sensitive summary (WMCC or expected cost with explicit C_FN, C_FP, C_rev), with the review as the unit of cross-review inference (per LLM4SCREENLIT).
- The L2D literature supplies the principled objective (expected cost with review cost and expert error), but the SR-screening literature almost never estimates expert error on deferred items; Hilkenmeier et al. and Delgado-Chaves et al. implicitly assume the human resolves deferred items correctly (as does the "Oracle-HITL" upper bound in Zhou & Li 2026).
- "Workload-matched comparison" (compare policies at equal human-review fraction) is implicit in WSS@R and in Figalova et al.'s retained-vs-recall table, but no LLM screening paper found plots a full curve across review fractions.

### Gaps
- No paper found that computes AURC/risk-coverage curves for LLM title-abstract screening; Rahgozar & Mortezaagha explicitly "does not report a recall-versus-human-review-fraction curve".
- The Phil Trans A conformal-LLM scoping review and the ACL SRW paper's full text could not be read (403 / binary PDF).
- Risk-coverage definitions from Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017 and the AURC definition from Geifman 2019 were confirmed only from abstracts/metadata in this session.

## Key Question 2: Is there published work where a failed verification (missing/invalid evidence or citation) is the abstention/deferral signal rather than model confidence?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only partially and mostly outside screening: citation-verification tools emit an explicit "uncertain" verdict and route it to human review (HALLMARK 2026); evidence-sufficiency benchmarks define abstention as the correct output when evidence is irrelevant/absent/conflicting (Zhang & Wu 2026; GRAB-RAG 2026); and fact-checking work trains or tests models to output NOT_ENOUGH_INFO when cited evidence is ablated (Deng et al. 2026). No paper was found that uses "the model's own citation/quote failed verification" as the runtime trigger for deferring a classification decision to a human in document screening; the closest analogues use agreement failure (multi-model disagreement) or classifier-uncertainty as the trigger. Searches for the literal phrases "cite-or-defer", "verification failure as uncertainty signal", "evidence-based abstention", and "grounding failure triage" returned no papers using those terms.

### Cited Findings
- HALLMARK (2026, anonymous, under review; arXiv 2607.18360): benchmarks LLM citation verifiers; abstentions are entries a tool "declines to label either way"; Coverage = fraction of entries a tool commits to; the bibtex-updater cascade routes single-source absence to "uncertain/human review" rather than flagging it, and the authors present this as the principled alternative to any-no-match flagging. Bibtex-updater: detection rate 0.865, FPR 0.092, F1 0.890, coverage 0.82 (abstains on ~20%); DeepSeek-R1 abstains on 21.7%; aggressive mode labels any non-verified entry hallucinated at confidence 0.55. At a venue-realistic ~2% base rate, precision is governed by FPR; guidance: high-recall verifiers plus human triage when "downstream human review absorbs false alarms". No measured human-review results. — [arXiv 2607.18360 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18360v1)
- Zhang & Wu (2026), "Do LLMs Know When Evidence is Insufficient? An Evidence Sufficiency Benchmark for Answer-Abstention Calibration in RAG" (Computers, Materials & Continua 89(1); DOI 10.32604/cmc.2026.086343): five-level gradient L1 full support, L2 partial, L3 irrelevant, L4 no context, L5 conflicting; L3-L5 require abstention. Metrics: abstention rate, over-answer rate (answered on L3-L5), accuracy on L1-L2, wrong-answer rate on L5, Behavioral ECE computed from answer/abstain decisions, and an "evidence sufficiency curve". Seven models: abstention < 10% at L1-L2, 27-67% at L3, 34-84% at L4, but only 9.5-34.8% at L5 (the "conflict trap"); L5 over-answer rates 65-91%; Claude Sonnet 4.6 highest accuracy (87.4%) but 69.8% over-answer. Human validation 89% agreement (75% at L5). — [CMC article](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v89n1/68467/html)
- Setiawan (2026, preprint), "Prompt-Based Abstention Fails Under Misleading Context" / GRAB-RAG (arXiv 2608.22228): paired benchmark (supportive, degraded, missing, misleading) on NQ and HotpotQA with three 3.8-8B frozen models; with explicit abstention prompting, answer rates stay <= 3% when evidence is missing but rise to 13.6-74.3% when a fluent passage supports a wrong answer. — [arXiv 2608.22228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22228) (abstract + search snippet)
- Deng et al. (2026), "Evaluating and Improving Evidence-Grounded Fact-Checking in LLMs via Multi-Round Evidence Ablation" (CIKM 2026; arXiv 2609.08943): Fact-Ablated Evaluation removes the verifier's cited evidence and re-queries for R = 4 rounds; a grounded verifier should switch to NOT_ENOUGH_INFO once its evidence is ablated. Metrics: Immediate Sensitivity (ISIS), End-State Retention (ERET), Ideal Offset (IOIO), label-flip rate. GPT-4o-mini ISIS 54.91 / ERET 78.21; Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite ISIS 75.15; Llama-3.1-8B ISIS 43.79; their REAL training (second input with gold evidence removed labelled NOT_ENOUGH_INFO) reaches ISIS 99.06. Authors position FAE as diagnostic, not a runtime abstention trigger. — [arXiv 2609.08943 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08943)
- Buchmann & Gurevych (2025), "Citation Failure: Definition, Analysis and Efficient Mitigation" (TACL; arXiv 2510.20303): disentangles citation failure (helpful response, incomplete citations) from response failure (response itself flawed, full citation impossible); studies how the response-evidence relation affects citation quality. — [arXiv 2510.20303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20303) (abstract only)
- Wallat et al. (2024), "Correctness is not Faithfulness in RAG Attributions" (arXiv 2412.18004; also ACM ICTIR 2025 DOI 10.1145/3731120.3744592): with Command-R+ on 1,444 NQ questions, appending an answer statement to a document caused the model to cite that document 12% of the time for random documents, 57% for relevant-but-uncited documents, and 55% for documents cited for other reasons; conclusion: a valid-looking citation cannot be taken as evidence the answer relied on the document; "cannot be taken as faithful by default". — [arXiv 2412.18004 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18004v1)
- Ranjan et al. (2026), "Rethinking LLM Verification: Evidence Structure, Uncertainty, and Selective Refinement" (arXiv 2608.10725): applied ontology grounding "only when the model abstains" and claimed abstained predictions have lower confidence; WITHDRAWN by the authors on 21 Aug 2026 ("submitted prematurely"); treat as unverified. — [arXiv 2608.10725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10725)
- Presacan et al. (2026) list "evidence insufficiency" triggers (knowledge-based abstention when a question cannot be answered from a verified fact base; text-to-SQL abstention when schema coverage is lacking) among uncertainty-driven abstention, but do not name "verification failure" or "grounding failure" as a separate category. — [Europe PMC full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13631354/fullTextXML)
- Agreement-failure as the deferral signal (the closest screening analogue): Hilkenmeier et al. (2026) accept only full three-LLM agreement (GPT-4.0, Gemini Pro 1.0, Claude 3 Sonnet) and route the rest (15-43% of records across six datasets) to humans; recall on auto-classified records 0.85-1.00. — [PMC13263951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263951/). Krag et al. (2024, medRxiv preprint, DOI 10.1101/2024.10.01.24314702): "two LLMs with disagreement solved by human arbitration" gave 91% automation at 8% error (lowest error rate reported) vs LLM pre-screening at 55% automation with no missed abstracts; automation/error-rate definitions not in the abstract. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22Large%20language%20models%20for%20abstract%20screening%20in%20systematic-%20and%20scoping%20reviews%22&format=json&resultType=core) (abstract only; full text returned 403)

### Inferences
- The field has the ingredients for "cite-or-defer" (verifier abstention verdicts, evidence-sufficiency gradients, ablation tests for grounding) but they live in RAG/fact-checking/citation-integrity venues, not in screening or clinical triage; a screening gate that defers when the model's quoted evidence fails a span-level check would be novel relative to everything found.
- Wallat et al. and Deng et al. jointly imply that a citation passing verification is weak evidence of a grounded decision (post-rationalisation), while a citation failing verification is a stronger signal of a problem; this asymmetry supports using verification failure as a deferral trigger but argues against treating verification success as licence to auto-accept.
- HALLMARK's pairing of abstention-excluded metrics with coverage is the right reporting pattern for any gate: report precision/recall on committed items alongside the fraction committed, and report an "aggressive" setting where unverified = flagged.

### Gaps
- No publication found that measures recall vs human-review fraction when the deferral trigger is a failed quote/citation check in title-abstract or full-text screening.
- Krag et al. 2024 full text (definitions of automation rate and error rate; whether arbitration cost is counted) could not be read.
- The literal query terms ("cite-or-defer", "evidence-based abstention", "grounding failure triage", "verification failure as uncertainty signal") returned no matching paper titles; the concept appears under "evidence sufficiency", "abstention calibration", "citation verifier abstention", and "NOT_ENOUGH_INFO".

## Key Question 3: In systematic review screening automation, which papers evaluate partial automation with human review of an uncertain band, and how do they count workload?

### Takeaway
Across 2024-2026, partial-automation designs fall into four families: (1) accept-on-agreement / defer disagreements (Hilkenmeier 2026; Krag 2024; Figalova 2026 unions); (2) LLM as replacement second reviewer with human adjudication of conflicts (Delgado-Chaves 2025; Sanghera 2025 proposed); (3) an explicit third label ("Unclear"/"unsure"/"MAYBE") retained for human or full-text review (Figalova 2026; Trad 2025; Rahgozar 2026); and (4) recall-targeted threshold calibration or active-learning stopping (Wang et al. 2024; Callaghan 2020; Boetje 2024). Workload is counted inconsistently: as records humans need not appraise (Sanghera), as manual classifications relative to dual screening (Hilkenmeier), as agreed records over original search yield (Delgado-Chaves), as retained-record percentage plus logged minutes (Figalova), as WSS@95 and token cost (Rahgozar), or as screening hours (Trad). None of the five workload-claiming papers surveyed by LLM4SCREENLIT priced false negatives.

### Cited Findings
- Rahgozar & Mortezaagha (2026, preprint; read in full): eight Cohen 2006 drug-class reviews (310-2,544 records; inclusion 1.6-27%); auxiliary BERT+GCN classifier flags MAYBE when |P(Include) - P(Exclude)| < 0.15 (14.5% MAYBE rate, 21.1% catch rate of includes, 1.46x lift; softmax-entropy variant 42.2% rate, 53.1% catch, 1.26x lift). Policies: baseline, full spectral context, decision-only, MAYBE-only (extra context only for MAYBE papers), and two-pass self-escalation (second call if pass-1 confidence LOW/MEDIUM or hedging regex). Results (8-dataset means): recall 0.91/0.92/0.91/0.92/0.90; WSS@95 0.47/0.52 (p = 0.039)/0.47/0.47/0.48; cost multiplier 1.00/1.28/1.12/1.05/1.00. Two-pass escalated 22.2% +/- 8.8% of records with a 0.0% flip rate across all datasets and folds ("hard anchoring limit"; "current instruction-tuned LLMs cannot self-triage"). LLM output was binary (no MAYBE allowed), listed as a limitation; the REASON field was not evaluated as a signal. — [arXiv 2608.14551 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14551)
- Figalova et al. (2026, preprint; read in full): 1,131-record benchmark from a 5,291-record gaze-semantics scoping review; four-way label Include / Unclear / Exclude / Exclude-citation-seed, with Unclear = "insufficient for a confident eligibility decision" and Include + Unclear both mapped to "retained"; LLMs were instructed to give Unclear rather than confident exclusion for ambiguous records. Operational recall vs retained: human workflows 82.3-82.9% recall at 42.2-45.0% retained; GPT-5.4 file batches ~82.6% at 42-45%; GPT-5.4 all-at-once 66.8% at 34.6%; Gemini 3 file batches 77.2% at 63.1%; Gemini 3.1 Pro file batches 83.9% at 56.7%; human union 98.1% recall at 57.2% retained; single reviewer + Gemini 3.1 union 98.1% at 67.4%. Two nominally identical GPT-5.4 runs agreed on 91.7% of records; 29 eligible records were discordant and 40 missed by both; union of the two runs 87.3% recall at 48.5% retained. Workflows NOT tested: multi-LLM agreement with human referral; LLM as one of two independent screeners. — [arXiv 2608.26885 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26885)
- Hilkenmeier, Stoltenberg & Stierle (2026, Systematic Reviews 15:191; read in full): full-agreement auto-classification on six datasets; auto-classified share 57% (Meijboom 2021) to 85% (authors' calibration sample); recall on auto-classified records 0.97, 1.00, 0.85, 0.88, 0.98, 0.99 and specificity 0.94, 0.72, 1.00, 0.97, 0.99, 0.98; workload 1,458 vs 5,028 manual classifications (>70% reduction); Gemini refused 15 records; 22 prompt iterations; Meijboom would have failed the stop criterion. — [PMC13263951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263951/)
- Delgado-Chaves et al. (2025, PNAS; read in full): 18 LLMs x 3 reviews (4,662 / 1,741 / 66 records; includes 185 / 122 / 45); per-criterion True/False with justification, include iff all criteria true; first human reviewer is the reference; agreements settle the record, disagreements and unclassifiable records go to a second human; mean agreement 92% / 88% / 40%; one-reviewer workload reduction 33-93%; no "uncertain" category (parser applies unspecified defaults; malformed outputs dropped); setup effort excluded from workload. — [PMC11745399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11745399/)
- Sanghera et al. (2025, JAMIA; read in full): 23 Cochrane reviews (Issue 8, 2023), 119,695 records; on the full set GPT-3.5 heavy prompt sensitivity 1.000 / specificity 0.419 / precision 0.004; GPT-4o extreme 0.904 / 0.949; Sonnet 3.5 none 0.823 / 0.982; best parallel ensemble with perfect sensitivity (GPT-3.5 heavy + Sonnet 3.5) specificity 0.419 and workload reduction 41.81%; highest workload reduction (Gemini 1.5 Pro + Sonnet 3.5 in series) 99.13% at sensitivity 0.690. Binary outputs only; errors/uninterpretable outputs labelled "include"; uncertainty flags left as future work; the "LLM as parallel second reviewer" (halving first-pass workload) is proposed, not tested. — [JAMIA](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/893/8090299)
- Trad et al. (2025, BMC Med Res Methodol 25:130; PMC12065358; abstract only): GPT-4 pipeline asks each screening question with yes/no/unsure and retains "unsure" articles ("If the model is uncertain, we retain the article ... improving sensitivity"); Vitamin D and falls SR, 14,439 articles; specificity 99.6%, FNR 0%; manual screening time reduced 95.5% (25.5 h total); Rayyan's "undecided" category counted as include gave 5% FNR; narrowing to "likely to exclude" gave 0% FNR at 81.3 h. — [arXiv 2412.15247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15247); [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1186/s12874-025-02583-5&format=json&resultType=core)
- Guo et al. - Wang et al. (2024), "Zero-shot Generative LLMs for Systematic Review Screening Automation" (ECIR 2024; arXiv 2401.06320; abstract + search extract): calibration sets an inclusion cutoff from a target recall, using seed studies or leave-one-topic-out extrapolation; ~50% of topics met the 0.95 recall target under extrapolation, success rate 0.56-1.00 when the calibration target is recall 1; calibrated Bio-SIEVE at recall 1: success 0.704, WSS 0.369 vs uncalibrated 0.111 / 0.858. — [arXiv 2401.06320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06320)
- Lee et al. (2026, J Epidemiol, DOI 10.2188/jea.je20260233; abstract only): GPT-5.0-mini 91% sensitivity at 96.7% workload reduction (core-subject, TBR 69%) and 83% at 92.7% (peripheral, TBR 4.4%); peripheral disagreement 10.6% (74/696) with 52/56 FNs = intended human leniency and true LLM misses 0.4% (3/696); calls for "appropriate safeguards for ambiguous records". — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42402393%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core)
- Mantyla et al. - Clinical triage analogue (read in full): Zhou & Li (2026, preprint), "Few-Shot LLMs for Actionable Triage Categorization of Online Patient Inquiries" (arXiv 2605.15680): auto-accept only when two models return the same valid label; "manual escalation rate" 32.0-42.7% across five pairs; "consensus accuracy" on auto-accepted 53.8-59.9%; Oracle-HITL macro-F1 0.655-0.708 (upper bound assuming humans resolve all escalations correctly); agreement reliable for self-care but not for urgent-clinician-review; under-triage 5.3-10.7%. — [arXiv 2605.15680 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15680)
- Strong, Men & Noble (2024/25, preprint v3; read in full), "Trustworthy and Practical AI for Healthcare: A Guided Deferral System with LLMs" (arXiv 2406.07212): lumbar spine MRI reports (OSCLMRIC, ~95% negative, 1,800 examples); deferral confidence = 2|p - 0.5| from verbalised probability, a hidden-state MLP probe, or their mean (alpha = 0.5); metric AUARC; hidden-state probe AUARC 0.9974; combined gives best F1 0.94; pilot (n = 20) deferred ~5% (30/540) and guidance improved human accuracy on deferred cases (p < 0.01). Deferral uses numeric probabilities only; rationale text is shown to humans, not used as a signal. — [arXiv 2406.07212 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07212)

### Inferences
- The best-documented "uncertain band" designs count workload as the fraction of records lacking full agreement (Hilkenmeier: 15-43%; Krag: ~9% at 8% error; Jain et al.: 14.6% routed) rather than as a confidence threshold on a single LLM; single-LLM verbalised confidence has not been shown to produce a usable band in screening (Rahgozar: 0% flip rate; Sanghera: binary only).
- Figalova et al.'s Unclear category behaves as a sensitivity lever (Unclear -> retained) rather than as a human-adjudication queue; this is the same semantics as Trad et al.'s "unsure" and Rayyan's "undecided", so these papers measure recall gain from retaining the band, not adjudication cost.
- Workload numbers across papers are not comparable without re-expressing them in a common unit (manual classifications per record vs records per reviewer vs hours); LLM4SCREENLIT's confusion-matrix mandate is the minimum needed to recompute them.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports a full recall-vs-human-review-fraction curve for an LLM screening gate, or that varies the size of the uncertain band and reports marginal recall per additional human-reviewed record.
- No paper found that measures human adjudication accuracy on deferred screening records (all assume the human is correct).
- Krag et al. 2024 (automation/error definitions) and the Phil Trans A conformal review were not readable; LLM4SCREENLIT R6-R9 were cut off in the fetched text.

## Key Question 4: Does LLM rationale quality correlate with decision correctness, such that rationale failure is a usable deferral signal?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin and mostly negative or unmeasured. The strongest adjacent results are: verbalised confidence tracks the model's willingness to commit more than correctness (Kumaran 2026); explanations from LLM error detectors are unreliable even when binary verdicts are right (Kamoi et al. 2024); citations can be correct yet post-rationalised (Wallat et al. 2024); showing explanations increases over-reliance (Bansal 2021; Jain et al. 2025); and in screening, correct rationales co-occur with wrong decisions on negated criteria (Mantyla 2026) while prompting for an explanation sometimes flips wrong decisions (Guo 2024). No paper was found that scores rationale quality on screening items and reports AUROC or risk-coverage for using it as a deferral signal.

### Cited Findings
- Kumaran (2026, preprint, Google DeepMind), "Reported Confidence in LLMs Tracks Commitment More Than Correctness" (arXiv 2606.29490; read in full): two-phase answer-then-commit/abstain paradigm on SimpleQA, MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA-hard, HLE across 8 models; correctness AUROC: calibrated log-prob 0.62-0.80 vs class-verbal confidence 0.53-0.65 and numeric-verbal 0.51-0.68; "decision-truth gap" (abstention AUROC minus correctness AUROC) median +0.13 for verbal confidence (positive in 7/8 non-reasoning cells, 15/16 reasoning cells) vs ~+0.02 for log-probs; after residualising on log-probs, verbal confidence predicts correctness at chance (AUROC 0.473) but still predicts abstention; Gemma 4 probes: decision AUROC ~0.94 vs correctness ~0.66, decision and correctness directions cosine -0.01. — [arXiv 2606.29490 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29490v1)
- Kamoi et al. (2024), "Evaluating LLMs at Detecting Errors in LLM Responses" / ReaLMistake (COLM 2024; arXiv 2404.03602; abstract + search extract): "explanations provided by LLM-based error detectors are unreliable", open models "often provide wrong reasoning even when the binary predictions are correct"; self-consistency, multi-LLM voting, and evaluation-step prompts did not improve error detection. — [arXiv 2404.03602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03602)
- Tan et al. (2025), "Too Consistent to Detect: A Study of Self-Consistent Errors in LLMs" (EMNLP 2025 main; arXiv 2505.17656; abstract only): self-consistent errors (same wrong answer across samples) do not diminish with scale; all four detector types drop substantially on them; consistency-based signals therefore have a blind spot. — [arXiv 2505.17656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17656)
- Wallat et al. (2024): citations that look correct are post-rationalised in up to 57% of adversarial cases (see KQ2). — [arXiv 2412.18004 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18004v1)
- Bansal et al. (2021, CHI): explanations increased acceptance of AI recommendations whether or not they were correct and did not produce complementary gains beyond confidence display. — [arXiv 2006.14779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779) (abstract only)
- Jain et al. (2025): "Displaying AI explanation, confidence, and labels leads to over-reliance, but just showing search results and evidence fosters more appropriate trust"; when the AI was wrong, showing judgments with evidence and reasoning dropped human accuracy from baseline (p < .001); evidence-only assistance raised accuracy when the AI was right (79.3% vs 71.3%) without harming it when the AI was wrong (64.0% vs 61.5%). — [arXiv 2510.26518 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26518v2)
- Mantyla et al. (2026): "In TP, an LLM often gave a correct rationale but still reached the wrong decision, mainly on negated exclusion criteria"; some rationales exposed human oversights; rationale-decision mismatch was not systematically quantified; confidence outputs clustered at 0.7 and 0.3, rarely 0.5. — [arXiv 2606.17588 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17588)
- Guo et al. (2024): on a random subset the models "corrected their initial decisions upon being asked to explain their reasoning for incorrect classifications" (no rate given in the abstract). — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e48996/)
- Rahgozar & Mortezaagha (2026): hedging terms in the REASON field triggered escalation in 22.2% of records but the second pass never changed a decision (0% flip), so rationale hedging as a self-triage signal was ineffective; rationale quality itself was not scored. — [arXiv 2608.14551 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14551)
- Strong, Men & Noble (2024/25): the model's generated for/against reasoning is given to humans as guidance (improving their accuracy on deferred cases, p < 0.01) but is not used in the deferral decision. — [arXiv 2406.07212 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07212)
- Machcha et al. (2026): chain-of-thought "can increase uncertainty without making abstention safer"; few-shot and CoT gave negligible abstention gains. — [arXiv 2601.12471 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12471)
- Verbalised-confidence baselines (abstract only): Tian et al. (2023, EMNLP), "Just Ask for Calibration": for RLHF models, verbalised confidence is better calibrated than token probabilities on TriviaQA/SciQ/TruthfulQA, "often reducing the expected calibration error by a relative 50%". — [arXiv 2305.14975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975). Xiong et al. (2024, ICLR), "Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty?": verbalised confidence is overconfident; consistency across samples and better aggregation help; white-box methods only slightly better (AUROC example 0.522 to 0.605); all methods struggle on professional-knowledge tasks. — [arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)

### Inferences
- The available evidence supports using rationale structure checks (e.g., does the rationale cite a criterion-relevant span; does it contradict the label; does the quoted span exist) as a deferral trigger only in the "fail -> defer" direction; a fluent rationale should not be treated as evidence of correctness (post-rationalisation, over-reliance, and commitment-not-correctness findings).
- Rationale-label contradiction on negated criteria (Mantyla 2026) and criterion-level Boolean combination errors are specific, checkable failure modes; programmatic checks of rationale-vs-label consistency are more promising than scoring rationale "quality" holistically.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures AUROC/AURC of a rationale-quality score (human- or model-rated) for predicting screening-decision correctness.
- No study found that tests "rationale cites a verifiable span" as a deferral rule in screening or clinical document classification.
- Lanham et al. (2023, Anthropic) "Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning" and "Evaluating Human Alignment and Model Faithfulness of LLM Rationale" (arXiv 2407.00219) appeared in search results but were not read; they concern CoT faithfulness in general, not screening.
- A "Learning to Defer: A Survey" by Strong et al. was mentioned in one search summary but could not be located on arXiv by title search; treat as unverified.
