# LLM-based title/abstract screening for systematic reviews and high-recall retrieval (TAR): primary literature as of October 2026

Scope note: screening/TAR only. Papers marked **[full text read]** were read beyond the abstract (arXiv HTML, Europe PMC XML, or extracted PDF). Papers marked **[abstract only]** could not be fetched beyond the abstract/landing page (several publisher sites returned 403). Nothing below is fabricated; where a detail could not be verified it is stated as such. Note on naming: the paper the assignment calls "Zhuang et al. 2024 arXiv:2401.06320" is first-authored by Shuai Wang (Zhuang is third author); it is cited here as Wang et al. 2024.

## KQ1. Strongest results and designs for zero-/few-shot LLM screening; which asked for explanations/evidence; did any verify them?

### Takeaway
The best-performing designs are (i) calibrated next-token-probability thresholding at a target recall (Wang et al. 2024), (ii) heavily engineered sensitivity-biased prompts or "framework chain-of-thought" prompts evaluated on 10–23 reviews (Cao et al. 2025; Sanghera et al. 2025), and (iii) multi-model agreement/ensembles (Sanghera 2025; Hilkenmeier 2026; Tolend 2026). Several papers ask the LLM for a reason/justification alongside the decision (Delgado-Chaves 2025; Rahgozar 2026; TiAb Review Plugin 2026; Han 2025 CoT; Guo 2024 and Matsui 2024 post hoc), but none of the screening papers mechanically or semantically verifies those rationales against the abstract or uses the verification to gate the decision; the only paper that does so (Windisch et al. 2026 "Show Your Work") is on trial-eligibility-scope classification, not screening.

**1. Wang S, Scells H, Zhuang S, Potthast M, Koopman B, Zuccon G. "Zero-shot Generative Large Language Models for Systematic Review Screening Automation." ECIR 2024 (findings track); arXiv:2401.06320. [full text read]** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06320 ; HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2
- Task: binary include/exclude of a candidate study given the review title (main experiments use title only; criteria/seed-study prompts in appendix). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Datasets: CLEF-2017 (50 DTA topics), CLEF-2018 (30 DTA), CLEF-2019-DTA (8), CLEF-2019-Intervention (40), and the Seed Collection (39 topics, 38 after removing a 1-relevant topic); standard train/test splits ignored because zero-shot. Label level (abstract vs content qrels) not explicitly stated in the paper. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Prompt: yes/no only; no rationale. The model never generates text; the score is S = P(yes) − P(no) from next-token probabilities, min–max normalised per topic. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Calibration: threshold θ chosen to hit target recall 0.95 or 1.0 either (a) leave-one-topic-out extrapolation from other topics in the same collection (θ = median score reaching target recall), or (b) from seed studies (lower θ to the lowest seed score). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Ensemble: CombSUM of LLaMA2-7b-ins, LLaMA2-13b-ins and BioBERT. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Metrics: precision, recall, F3, balanced accuracy, "success rate" (fraction of topics reaching recall ≥0.95), WSS computed at r = 1 (i.e. WSS@100); no WSS@95, no MAP. Cost only as motivation (projected US$4,000 GPT-3.5 / US$80,000 GPT-4 to run all collections). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Key numbers: uncalibrated LLaMA2-7b-ins on CLEF-2017 P 0.08 / R 0.87 / WSS 0.56; calibrated ensemble @0.95 target on CLEF-2017 R 0.93, success 0.52, WSS 0.50; vs Bio-SIEVE on 27 held-out CLEF topics: Bio-SIEVE R 0.576 / success 0.111 vs calibrated ensemble R 0.937 / success 0.519 (target 0.95) and R 0.981 / success 0.889 (target 1.0). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)
- Topic-level validation: paired two-tailed t-test across topics with Bonferroni correction. Single deterministic run; no repeated runs, no variance; batching not discussed. Guanaco-7b labelled everything relevant. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06320v2)

**2. Dennstädt F, Zink J, Putora PM, Hastings J, Cihoric N. "Title and abstract screening for literature reviews using large language models: an exploratory study in the biomedical domain." - Models: FlanT5-XXL, OpenHermes-2.5-neural-chat-7B, Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, Platypus2-70B-Instruct; parameters set to deterministic (temperature 0) explicitly for reproducibility. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02575-4)

Label semantics explicitly flagged: in the published datasets only publications finally included in the SLR are labelled relevant, whereas the new dataset labels every publication that should be read in full text ("qualitatively different"). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02575-4)
- Results (3+ classifiers, 10 datasets): sensitivity/specificity 94.5%/31.8% FlanT5, 97.6%/19.1% OHNC, 81.9%/75.2% Mixtral, 97.2%/38.3% Platypus2; range per dataset from 100% sens at 87.9% spec (Mixtral, Wolters_2018) to 24.0% sens (Mixtral, Jeyaraman_2021); on the new dataset 100% sensitivity at specificity 12.6%/4.5%/62.5%/24.7%. Minor prompt wording and scale changes had "considerable impact". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02575-4)
- Metrics: sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, precision, F1, ROC; no WSS; no repeated runs; discusses hallucination only as a general risk. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02575-4)

**7. Matsui K, Utsumi T, Aoki Y, Maruki T, Takeshima M, Takaesu Y. ## KQ2. Novelty flags: does any paper already (a) measure rationale/quote verification failure in screening, (b) compare joint decision+rationale vs decision-only prompting on screening accuracy, or (c) route uncertain/unsupported decisions to a human queue with workload accounting?

### Takeaway
(a) No screening paper measures how often LLM rationales or quotations fail verification; the closest is Windisch et al. 2026 (eligibility-scope classification of 200 oncology RCT abstracts), which does measure it and should be cited as prior art for the mechanism. (b) Partial prior art exists: Han et al. 2025 (CoT with a Reasoning field vs zero-shot, batched) and Huotala et al. 2024 (few-shot CoT vs few-shot) compare reasoning vs non-reasoning prompts on screening accuracy, and Windisch 2026 compares label-only vs label+quote on classification accuracy and coverage; none isolates "joint decision+rationale vs decision-only" with rationale verification. (c) Routing with workload accounting exists in non-verification forms: Delgado-Chaves 2025 (unclassifiable + LLM/human conflicts → second reviewer, 33–93% reduction), Hilkenmeier 2026 (three-LLM disagreement → human, ~20% of records), and conceptually LLM4SCREENLIT R6/R10; no paper routes decisions to humans because the rationale/evidence failed verification.

## KQ3. Benchmarks and label semantics (CLEF TAR, SYNERGY, Cohen 2006, Cochrane-based sets); abstract-level vs content-level labels

### Takeaway

## KQ4. How workload is charged (WSS, recall@budget, stopping rules) and whether any paper costs an LLM "uncertain/needs review" category

### Takeaway
LLM screening papers mostly report WSS@95 or ad hoc "workload reduction" (= records the LLM excluded, or TN/N), sometimes API cost per record; the TAR literature separately provides statistically grounded stopping rules (Cormack & Grossman 2016; Callaghan & Müller-Hansen 2020; Yang/Lewis/Frieder 2021; Lewis et al. 2021; Li & Kanoulas 2020), and a 2026 evaluation of 15 stopping methods on 81 datasets finds only one reliably meets its recall target. The human cost of an LLM "uncertain" bucket is quantified only indirectly (Delgado-Chaves: conflicts + unclassifiable; Hilkenmeier: ~20% discordant).

### Cited Findings
- WSS definition: Cohen 2006 introduced "work saved over sampling at 95% recall". — [Source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1197/jamia.M1929&format=json&resultType=core); CLEF 2019 formula WSS@r = (TN+FN)/N − (1−r). — [Source](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/71253/1/Kanoulas_etal_CEUR_2019_CLEF_2019_technology_assisted_reviews_in_empirical_medicine_overview.pdf)
- Sanghera 2025 "workload reduction" = correctly excluded per 100 screened (TN/N); Homiar 2025 = records the LLM excluded, excluding prompt-development time; Sciurti 2025 "workload saving" = correctly excluded / total; Delgado-Chaves 2025 = second-reviewer records avoided (unclassified + conflicts remain). — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12012331/); [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12306261/); [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12873614/); [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11745399/)

- Callaghan et al. 2024 "Computer-assisted screening in systematic evidence synthesis requires robust and well-evaluated stopping criteria." Systematic Reviews 13:284. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02699-7)
- Spillias 2026 applies a pre-registered "SAFE" stopping rule on LLM-ensemble rankings (≥95% recall on all 10 reviews; 56% of corpus screened). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.29.741127)
- Hilkenmeier 2026 is the only paper where an LLM-side "needs review" set (disagreement, ~1 in 5 records) is the explicit human workload. — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263951/)

### Inferences
- No LLM screening paper combines a verification-driven "needs review" bucket with WSS@95-style accounting; disagreement-based routing (Hilkenmeier; Delgado-Chaves) is the nearest template and reports 7–20% of records routed.
- The TAR stopping literature (Repke 2026) implies that any LLM-ranked pipeline must be paired with a validated stopping rule to realise simulated savings.

### Gaps
- Yang/Lewis/Frieder 2021 and Lewis 2021 full texts not read (recall targets/datasets unverified beyond abstracts).

## KQ5. Instability at temperature 0 / repeated runs, and batching effects

### Takeaway
Repeated-run instability is small but non-zero at temperature 0 (Oami 2025: 95.9–98.9% agreement across three runs; Homiar 2025: 1/730 flips on included records; Figalová 2026: 91.7% record-level agreement between two nominally identical runs with 29 eligible records retained by only one), and batching multiple abstracts per prompt materially changes behaviour (Fagerberg 2026: GPT-5 mini sensitivity 0.88 at batch 200 → 0.48 at batch 400; Figalová: all-at-once < file-batch; Hida 2026: batch effects vary with prevalence).

### Cited Findings
- Syriani 2023: 10 runs, Fleiss' kappa high; recall identical across runs, specificity fluctuates slightly. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06464)
- Matsui 2024: temperature >0 (even 0.1) produced varying and malformed outputs; temperature 0 "does not always guarantee absolute uniformity". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.2196/52758)
- Huotala 2024: temperature 0 and seed 128, "some variation remains". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15667)
- Sanghera 2025: temperature 0.2, 3 repeats, median kappa 0.868–1.000. — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12012331/)
- Oami 2025 RSM: triplicate; consistency 98.9% GPT-4o, 97.8% Gemini 1.5 Pro, 95.9% Claude 3.5 Sonnet, 98.0% Llama 3.3 70B. — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12657656/)
- Homiar 2025: 50 records × 10 reruns; 73 included records × 10 → 1/730 erroneous exclusion; occasional contradictory (both included and excluded) outputs. — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12306261/)
- Figalová N, Huestegge L, Böckler-Raettig A. arXiv:2608.26885 (2026): preregistered; 1,131 records, 316 verified eligible; two nominally identical GPT-5.4 file-batch runs agreed on 91.7% of records, differing on 94, 29 of them eligible; all-at-once configurations recovered fewer eligible records than file batches; no workflow (human or LLM) found all 316. Temperature not reported. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26885)
- Fagerberg P, Sallander O, Patil KV, Berg A, Nyman A, Borg N, Lindén T. "Batch Size Effects on Mid-2025 State-of-the-Art LLM Performance in Automated Title and Abstract Screening." Cochrane Evid Synth Methods 2026, DOI 10.1002/cesm.70082 (medRxiv 2025.09.02.25334159): 790 references (93 relevant) from a Cochrane stem-cell/AMI review; batches 1–790; 10 repeated runs per model-batch; Gemini 2.5 Pro processed 790 in one batch; GPT-5 failed at ≥400; GPT-5 mini sensitivity 0.88 (batch 200) → 0.48 (batch 400); at batch 100 Gemini 2.5 Pro sensitivity 1.00, GPT-5 specificity 0.98; Gemini 2.5 Flash low sensitivity at batch 1. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/cesm.70082)
- Hida GS, Ribeiro DM, Hida CS. "Class Imbalance and Batch Effects in LLM-Based Screening for Systematic Reviews." arXiv:2608.14737 (ENIAC/BRACIS 2026): five reviews; prevalence metadata had limited influence; batch processing produced larger behavioural changes varying with prevalence; aggregate and item-level analyses disagree; batching should be assessed for decision effects, not only cost. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14737)
- Khraisha 2024 batched 3–5 abstracts per message in the ChatGPT UI; Han 2025 batches title/abstract pairs per prompt; Hilkenmeier 2026 processed rows individually; Delgado-Chaves embedded in batches of 100 but classified records individually. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17526v2); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16091); [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263951/); [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11745399/)
- Rahgozar 2026 notes temperature 0 "prevents analysis of run-to-run variance" but varies seeds across folds; Windisch 2026 at default temperature 1.0 found label Fleiss' kappa 0.829–0.969 across 3 repeats. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14551); [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13252682/)
- LLM4SCREENLIT: only two of 29 studies examined output consistency; R6 asks for consistency and null-output reporting. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12635v2)
- Search-only (not read): "Is one run enough? Reproducibility of flagship LLMs across temperature and reasoning settings in biomedical text processing" (PMC13197179, 2026). — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13197179/)

### Inferences
- Record-level agreement across repeated identical runs is typically 92–99%, so flips concentrate on borderline records; because relevant records are rare, even 1–2% flips can be a non-trivial share of includes (29/316 in Figalová).
- Batching is a confound that most benchmarks ignore; single-record prompting remains the safest evaluation default.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies instability of generated rationales/quotes across runs in screening (Windisch 2026 measures word-level Jaccard of quotes across runs, but for eligibility classification).

## KQ6. LLM screening in psychiatry / mental health specifically

### Takeaway
Several psychiatry-specific screening evaluations exist: Matsui 2024 (bipolar disorder, JMIR), Homiar 2025 (anhedonia living review, BMJ Mental Health), Tolend 2026 (autism/ADHD/PTSD, Can J Psychiatry), Dobin 2026 (suicide prevention, medRxiv), Parkington 2026 (digital psychiatry, PLOS Digital Health), and van de Schoot 2025 (PTSS trajectories, Eur J Psychotraumatology); the TiAb Review Plugin was tuned on a depression dataset.

### Cited Findings
- Matsui 2024: two bipolar-disorder reviews; GPT-4 adjusted sensitivity 0.962/0.943. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.2196/52758)
- Homiar 2025: anhedonia LSR, GPT-4o, 100% sensitivity for final includes, 65–85% workload reduction. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjment-2025-301762)
- Tolend 2026: ASD, ADHD, PTSD datasets; recall 97.0/100/96.4%; WSS@95 38.1/17.3/18.5%. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/07067437261445767)
- A companion "Hybrid Delphi-Inspired Expert-LLM Workflow for Efficient Evidence Screening" (Stud Health Technol Inform 2026, DOI 10.3233/shti260255) exists — not read. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3233/shti260255)
- Dobin 2026 ScreenAgent (suicide prevention): 201,064 records, sensitivity 97.7%, workload reduction 99.4%, $855.91 total. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.12.26360335)
- Parkington K, et al. PLOS Digit Health 2026, DOI 10.1371/journal.pdig.0001666: three GPT-5 modes vs consensus human workflow on a registered digital-psychiatry review (PROSPERO CRD42025648122) across nine SLR tasks including screening, with "structured hallucination auditing" finding no fabricated study-level content. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001666)
- van de Schoot R, et al. Eur J Psychotraumatol 2025, DOI 10.1080/20008066.2025.2546214: PTSS-trajectory review; human + active learning + LLM quality control; AI-aided methods added 3,822 records and 10 unique relevant studies. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008066.2025.2546214)
- TiAb Review Plugin tuned on a depression dataset (1,993 records, 14.1% prevalence; recall 96.1%, specificity 86.3%). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08602)
- Sanghera 2025 and Delgado-Chaves 2025 are not psychiatry-specific; Tolend reports the five-LLM Delphi matched or was beaten by the best single LLM on the two low-prevalence psychiatry sets. — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13133002/)

### Inferences
- Psychiatry corpora show the same pattern as general medicine (high sensitivity, low precision, large between-review variance); none of the psychiatry papers verifies rationales.

### Gaps
- Dobin 2026, Parkington 2026 and the SHTI Delphi paper were verified at abstract level only.
