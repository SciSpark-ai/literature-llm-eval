# Evidence-gated screening composes known parts into an untested whole

**Verdict on novelty.** No located work answers the candidate question; the closest single item is Windisch et al. 2026 ("Show Your Work"), which implements a label-plus-verbatim-quote prompt on 200 oncology RCT abstracts with an exact-substring validity check and reports that **mechanically valid quotes appear in only 83.3-91.2% of runs**, that **only 48.0-78.8% of evidence-bearing predictions are judged semantically supported**, and that requiring evidence lowers coverage by **1.5-5.5 points** and significantly reduces majority-vote accuracy for one of three models (Claude Opus 4.5, 0.785 to 0.725, McNemar p = 0.009) ([medRxiv full text](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt); [PMC13252682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13252682/)). The components of the candidate question are each established somewhere: exact-match quote-failure rates on biomedical and veterinary text (Windisch; VetScore), tiered exact/normalized/elided matchers and the lexical-versus-semantic support funnel (Verifiable by Construction; VetScore; GRACE), the accuracy cost of faithfulness-by-construction and of joint versus pipelined rationalisation (FRESH; Wiegreffe et al.), the recovery of accuracy by decoupling reasoning from formatting (Tam et al.; Format Tax), disagreement-based routing to humans with workload counted in classifications or person-days (Hilkenmeier; Delgado-Chaves; Fagerberg), cost-sensitive deferral with an explicit review cost (Singh et al.), and the Lost-Evidence/cost-sensitive reporting standard for screening (LLM4SCREENLIT). What is not established anywhere in the notes is (a) the positive-class recall lost under a *hard* "no verified quote, no positive" gate on a screening or monitoring task with relevance labels, (b) a same-task comparison of joint single-call quoting against two-call decoupling or sentence-ID/span selection under the same gate, (c) routing of verification failures (rather than model disagreement or low confidence) to a human queue with the queue costed, and (d) per-admitted-positive cost accounting with topic-level validation across reviews. The verdict carries explicit uncertainty: forward-citation chasing from Windisch et al. (March 2026 preprint) was not performed; ACL Anthology 2026 proceedings, GitHub READMEs of screening tools, and vendor technical reports (Elicit, Consensus, Scite, Undermind, OpenEvidence) were not searched directly; the full texts of Cao et al. 2025 (Annals, 403), Krag et al. 2024, Arabzadeh & Clarke 2025, the Phil. Trans. A conformal-LLM review, and the tail sections of Fagerberg et al. 2026 and LLM4SCREENLIT could not be read, and Windisch et al. remains a preprint (also indexed in Cureus/PMC) whose per-class recall under a gate is not reported.

## Four literatures each hold one piece and none holds the composition

The adversarial search that targeted the candidate question directly concluded that **"no item found combines all components"** and that the pieces live in separate venues: quote-validity measurement in biomedical classification (Windisch), excerpt matching in long-form QA (VetScore), quote-recall loss versus document length in public-comment processing (Raab et al.: sentence-level quote recall **17.9-47.0%**, precision 58-91%, declining with source length), referred-back accounting in screening methodology (LLM4SCREENLIT), prospective workload accounting for screening without quotes (Fagerberg et al.: **415 to 34 person-days**, inference spend about USD 400, 0/600 audited AI-exclusions confirmed as false negatives), and span-index citation as an engineering alternative to free-text quoting (Anthropic Citations) ([adversarial note, KQ1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt); [Raab et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08109); [Fagerberg et al.](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.02.26354724v1.full); [LLM4SCREENLIT](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12635v2); [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)).

The attribution literature supplies the single most important structural finding for the candidate design: the lexical gate is cheap and removes few decisions for frontier models, while the semantic-support gate is where decisions are actually lost. Verifiable by Construction reports that strong models lose almost nothing at the verbatim gate (gpt-5-mini 100.0 to 99.1% of claims; claude-opus-5 98.0 to 98.0%) while weak models lose 13-19 points (gpt-4.1-mini 49.6 to 36.3%), yet **Certified Claim Rate (verbatim and fully supported) ranges from 13.2% to 78.3%**, and claude-opus-5 sits at 37.1% despite 98% verbatim compliance; letting a judge search for any supporting span recovers most of the gap (37.1 to 85.1%), meaning many claims were supportable but the model chose a non-sufficient quote ([arXiv 2609.15964v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15964v2)). VetScore shows the same ordering of failure causes, formatting and normalization, then ellipsis assembly, then faithful paraphrase, then fabrication, with exact-match rates from **93.3% (Gemini 3.1 Pro) to 41.5% (Mistral Small)** and GPT-5 rising from 80.2% to 92.6% once ellipsis-bridged fragments are accepted ([arXiv 2608.03675](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03675)). Two implications follow for the candidate question. First, the recall loss attributable to extraction or verification failure is dominated by the choice of matcher tier and by whether the gate demands semantic support; an exact-only gate on a frontier model will cost only a few points, which is roughly what Windisch observed. Second, the design must separate "quote not in source" from "quote in source but insufficient," since Wallat et al. show that a passing citation can be post-rationalised (a model cited a document containing an appended answer statement **57% of the time** for relevant-but-uncited documents), so verification success is weak evidence of a grounded decision while verification failure is a stronger signal of trouble ([arXiv 2412.18004](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18004v1)).

The joint-versus-decoupled question has a long fine-tuning-era history that the LLM era has not revisited for evidence gates. FRESH's three-component pipeline (support model, extractor, independent classifier trained only on the rationale) costs **0-9 macro-F1 points** against a full-text classifier (SST .81 vs .90; near zero on Movies and MultiRC) while beating end-to-end rationale learning in both accuracy and stability ([ar5iv 2005.00115](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.00115)); Wiegreffe et al. find joint rationalisation costs about 0.1 points on e-SNLI and 4-6 points on CoS-E, while the explain-then-predict pipeline loses a further 1.7 and 10-11 points ([ar5iv 2010.12762](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.12762)). The LLM-era analogue is "reason freely, then format": Tam et al.'s NL-to-Format condition performed "nearly identically" to unrestricted text, and the Format Tax preprint attributes most loss to format-requesting instructions and reports that a two-pass reformat "substantially" recovers accuracy ([Tam et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442); [Format Tax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03616)). The notes are explicit that **no LLM-era paper runs "call 1: label; call 2: extract supporting quote" and reports the label shift against a single joint call**, and that no published head-to-head cost benchmark of the two designs exists; the only concrete cost table (Cohen et al. 2025, intent extraction) prices a decomposed pipeline at roughly **2-3x** a single end-to-end call (input 1,839 to 2,103 tokens, output 20 to 622, latency 0.24 to 0.6 s) ([arXiv 2509.12423](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12423)).

The screening literature routes to humans by disagreement, not by verification failure, and it counts workload inconsistently. Hilkenmeier et al. automate only full three-LLM agreement (57-85% of records), send the rest to a human adjudicator, and count workload as **1,458 versus 5,028 manual classifications** ([PMC13263951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263951/)); Delgado-Chaves et al. send unclassifiable and conflicting records to a second reviewer and report 33-93% second-reviewer reduction ([PMC11745399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11745399/)); Rahgozar & Mortezaagha escalate hedging records to a second LLM pass that flipped **0.0%** of decisions and explicitly do not report a recall-versus-review-fraction curve ([arXiv 2608.14551](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14551)); Figalová et al. treat "Unclear" as retained rather than as an adjudication queue and log human minutes per record ([arXiv 2608.26885](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26885)). LLM4SCREENLIT's survey finds that **none of the five papers claiming workload savings priced false negatives** and recommends that unclassifiable outputs be counted as positives requiring human review rather than dropped ([arXiv 2511.12635v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12635v2)). The selective-prediction note states plainly that no paper was found that uses "the model's own citation/quote failed verification" as the runtime trigger for deferring a screening decision to a human, nor any that reports AURC or risk-coverage curves for LLM title-abstract screening ([Singh et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27143); [Wen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18418)).

## Explicit novelty uncertainties

The claims the notes do not license are the following. The notes do not support a claim to be "first to measure exact-match verbatim-quote failure on scientific abstracts," because Windisch et al. report 8.8-16.7% invalid-quote rates on oncology RCT abstracts with GPT-5-era models; a safer claim is the first measurement of how span-length and contiguity instructions, and the matcher normalisation tier, change gate-induced loss, since no source tests a "short contiguous span" instruction and Windisch et al. list "multiple short spans, character offsets, or constrained decoding" only as future work ([adversarial note KQ2](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.03.26346690v1.full.txt)). The notes do not support a claim that joint single-call quoting is known to be better or worse than two-call decoupling for LLMs; the closest evidence is the fine-tuning-era ordering (predict-only >= joint > pipeline) and the LLM-era "reason then format" results, plus one NAACL Findings 2024 data point (Yeo et al.) favouring joint on faithfulness, none of which involves a verified quote gate ([Yeo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11863v3)). The notes do not support a claim that verification failure is a validated deferral signal; no paper reports risk-coverage or selective-accuracy curves for "defer when the quote fails verification," and the nearest analogues (Ye & Durrett calibrators; Patil's support-contra asymmetry; Kumaran's finding that verbal confidence tracks commitment rather than correctness) are unvalidated as selective-prediction policies ([Patil](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21884v2); [Kumaran](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29490v1)). The notes do not support any claim about GPT-4-class (2023-2024) exact-match rates on abstracts, since all located figures are for GPT-5-era models.

## Reusable methodological standards

**Label semantics.** CLEF TAR ships two qrel levels per topic: abstract-level (Included plus Excluded-at-article-stage) and content-level (Included only), with abstract-level prevalence 1.88%/1.58% (dev/test) versus document-level 0.52%/0.33% in 2017 and 12.9%/5.3% (DTA) in 2019 ([CLEF 2017](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1866/invited_paper_12.pdf); [CLEF 2019](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/71253/1/Kanoulas_etal_CEUR_2019_CLEF_2019_technology_assisted_reviews_in_empirical_medicine_overview.pdf)). ASReview-repository and SYNERGY datasets label final inclusions only, which Dennstädt et al. call "qualitatively different" from TiAb-stage labels; Homiar et al. report 100% sensitivity against final inclusions but 58-100% against human TiAb inclusions; Sciurti et al. compute every metric against both references ([Dennstädt](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02575-4); [Homiar](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12306261/); [Sciurti](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12873614/)). Nunnery 2026 formalises why: reference sets are workflow-induced, so a gated workflow should state which adjudication produced its labels. The FEVER family's handling of insufficiency maps directly onto a gate: NEI is scored on label alone with no evidence requirement, and the gate should distinguish "no evidence found" from "evidence found but insufficient" ([FEVER](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.05355v3); [SciFact](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.14974v6)).

**Workload metrics.** The convention set is WSS@r = (TN + FN)/N - (1 - r) at r = 0.95 (Cohen 2006; CLEF 2019); "workload reduction" as correctly excluded per 100 records (Sanghera) or records the LLM excluded (Homiar); manual classifications relative to dual screening (Hilkenmeier, 1,458 vs 5,028); second-reviewer records avoided (Delgado-Chaves); retained percentage plus logged minutes per record (Figalová, 1.04-1.44 min); person-days at 1 min/record and 6 h/day (Fagerberg, 415 to 34); token-cost multipliers (Rahgozar, 1.28x) and USD per 1,000 abstracts (Han, 0.09-3.04). LLM4SCREENLIT adds Lost Evidence (1 - Recall) as primary, MCC, weighted MCC with w = 10, a justified FN:FP ratio (Khraisha used 30x), a predefined Lost-Evidence deployment threshold, and full confusion matrices per model, dataset, and prompt. Singh et al. supply the cost objective (misclassification terms plus abstention rate times C_rev, with a noisy-expert correction and a break-even C_rev*); HALLMARK's pattern of reporting precision and recall on committed items alongside the fraction committed is the matching report format for a gate. No located paper plots recall against human-review fraction; a candidate study should.

**Statistical unit.** LLM4SCREENLIT treats the review as the unit for cross-review inference and warns that within-review standard error on a full labelled population is effectively zero, so binomial or bootstrap CIs on item-level data are inappropriate. Practice follows: Wang et al. use paired t-tests across topics with Bonferroni correction and a topic-level success rate; Rahgozar uses paired Wilcoxon across eight datasets; SESR-Eval reports that between-review variance exceeds between-LLM variance ([SESR-Eval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19027)); Sanghera's per-review R^2 of 0.196 between human and LLM sensitivity shows why pooled figures mislead.

**Judge validation protocols.** The alt-test requires at least three human annotators and 50-100 items and defines an Average Advantage Probability ([Calderon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10970)). IR practice reports label-level Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha with CIs (Thomas: +/-0.02 on ~3,000 pairs) against a human-human bar (trained TREC assessors kappa 0.52; crowd 0.24-0.52; lab 0.58; two human judgment sets tau 0.90) and per-class precision, since LLMs over-assign relevance (non-relevant precision 0.82-0.98 versus relevant 0.39-0.69 in Alaofi et al.) ([Thomas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10621); [Alaofi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17969)). Blinding is enforced by labelling the validation sample without the LLM label (from-scratch human labels matched GPT-4o 56% versus 72% for post-edited labels in Thakur et al.) and by seeding randomly flipped labels to measure rubber-stamping (Dietz et al., trope #12) ([Thakur](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15205); [Dietz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19076)). Prediction-powered inference builds CIs from LLM labels plus a small reliable sample ([Oosterhuis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02464)), and Dorner et al. bound the label savings at 2x when the judge is no better than the evaluated model. Prompt paraphrases alone move kappa by about 0.1-0.2, so any gate-prompt change must be re-validated on the same gold sample.

**Matcher tiers.** Verifiable by Construction defines Exact (character-for-character), Normalized (lowercase, Unicode canonical form, curly quotes and dashes folded, whitespace collapsed, markup stripped), and Elided (ellipsis with ordered fragments, fragments under 10 characters ignored, at least two remaining); GRACE adds "assembled" (non-contiguous verbatim fragments) and "partial" (at least half of fragments) tiers; VetScore uses exact, ellipsis-aware fuzzy, and an LLM classifier into formatting/paraphrase/hallucination validated on 100 manual cases; ResearchQA rejects embedding similarity because it "would pass a fabricated-but-on-topic quote"; Windisch uses whitespace-normalised substring; ERASER's token-IOU threshold of 0.5 is the extractive-rationale precedent for tolerating an extra token ([VbC](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15964v2); [GRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23726); [ResearchQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11074v1); [ERASER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03429)). Ratio-based fuzzy thresholds (85-90) appear only in practitioner posts and should not be cited as validated. Ellipsis use is model-specific, so a gate that forbids ellipses penalises some models unless the instruction specifies a single contiguous span.

**Support grading.** LongCite scores citation recall per statement as 0/0.5/1 (no, partial, full support) with a GPT-4o judge and reports citation length as a granularity measure ([LongCite](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02897v3)); ALCE's human protocol uses fully/partially/does-not-support with precision credit for partial support; Verifiable by Construction uses fully/partially/not supported/contradicted with strict (fully) and lenient (fully or partially) rates and a majority-of-three judge, validated by two annotators on 70 pairs (kappa 0.853); AIS uses a binary two-stage protocol at the whole-output level; AttrScore uses attributable/extrapolatory/contradictory. The Windisch two-tier gate (mechanical validity, then LLM-judged support) is the direct template, and its result that 21-52% of mechanically valid quotes fail support shows the second tier cannot be omitted.

**Repeated-run reporting.** Temperature 0 is low-variance, not zero-variance (Atil et al.; Matsui; Huotala), so studies report consistency across repeats: Windisch 3 runs with Fleiss kappa on labels (0.829-0.969) and word-level Jaccard on quotes (0.664-0.897); Oami triplicate agreement 95.9-98.9%; Homiar 1/730 erroneous exclusions on 10 reruns of included records; Sanghera 3 repeats with median kappa 0.868-1.000; Figalová 91.7% agreement between two identical runs with 29 eligible records discordant ([Oami](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12657656/); [Atil](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667)). Batch composition is a separate confound (GPT-5 mini sensitivity 0.88 to 0.48 between batch sizes 200 and 400; Hida et al. show item-level and aggregate analyses disagree), so single-record prompting with recorded model snapshot, seed, and provider defaults is the evaluation default, and quote stability across runs should be reported alongside label stability because no screening paper has done so.

## Conclusion

The literature has matured to the point where the candidate question is no longer about whether LLM quotes fail verification (they do, at 6-17% for frontier models on biomedical text and far more for small models) but about where the loss sits and who pays for it. The attribution funnels show that an exact-match gate on a frontier model forfeits only a few points, while a semantic-support gate forfeits tens of points and the difference is largely "supportable claim, wrong quote chosen," which is precisely the failure that a second extraction call or a sentence-ID selection step is positioned to repair. That reframes the candidate study: its most valuable result is likely to be a decomposition of gate-induced loss into matcher-tier, span-choice, and support-adjudication components, with each component's human-review cost priced per retained relevant record.

The second implication is that the screening field's routing habit (defer disagreement, assume the human is right, leave the queue uncosted) is the weakest link, and the candidate design's "failures routed to a costed human review queue" is where it can set a standard rather than merely fill a gap. Combining LLM4SCREENLIT's confusion-matrix and Lost-Evidence mandate, Singh's expected-cost objective with an explicit C_rev, HALLMARK's committed-versus-abstained reporting, and a recall-versus-review-fraction curve that no screening paper yet plots would make the result interpretable across the inconsistent workload units now in use. The one precondition is to cite and distinguish Windisch et al. explicitly, and to check its forward citations before submission.
